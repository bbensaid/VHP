Chapter 20. The AHS Restructuring Roadmap. A Six-Pillar Framework for System Redesign.

We have reached the final chapter of the book. Chapter 20 is the most prescriptive chapter. It is written directly for the state leaders executing Vermont's Act 68 agenda. The chapter takes everything that has come before, the framework, the pillar chapters, the cross-cutting infrastructure chapters, and the political sustainability chapter, and applies it to a specific Vermont case. The case is the restructuring of the Vermont Agency of Human Services.

The chapter is organized around five elements. The strategic case for restructuring. The six-pillar restructuring map. The sequencing decisions inside the restructuring. The workforce transition plan. And the stakeholder alignment work that the restructuring requires.

Element one. The strategic case for restructuring.

The Vermont Agency of Human Services is the umbrella state agency that oversees the Department of Vermont Health Access, the Department of Health, the Department of Mental Health, the Department of Disabilities, Aging, and Independent Living, the Department for Children and Families, and the Department of Corrections. Together, these departments administer the substantial majority of Vermont's health, behavioral health, social services, and human services programs.

The current Agency structure was assembled over decades through accretion. New programs were added. Existing programs were occasionally reorganized. The boundaries between departments reflect the political and administrative history of Vermont state government as much as the operational logic of an integrated health and human services system.

The transformation strategy under Act 68 places operational demands on the Agency that the existing structure cannot fully support. Hospital global budgets require coordination across the Department of Vermont Health Access, the Department of Health, and the Department of Mental Health. Whole-person care under the Blueprint requires coordination across multiple departments. The Designated Agency system requires coordination across the Department of Mental Health and the Department of Disabilities, Aging, and Independent Living. The Rural Health Transformation Program requires coordination across all of the departments and several non-state institutional partners.

The case for restructuring is that the Agency's current departmental structure is, for the demands of Act 68 implementation, structurally suboptimal. Restructuring is not a goal in itself. It is a precondition for operationally credible Act 68 implementation.

Element two. The six-pillar restructuring map.

The restructuring can be mapped against the six-pillar framework.

Policy pillar implications. The restructuring must preserve and strengthen the Agency's policy capacity. The capacity includes legislative engagement, federal-state interface management, regulatory coordination with the Green Mountain Care Board, and section 1115 waiver management. The restructured Agency should have stronger centralized policy capacity than the current distributed model.

Economics pillar implications. The restructuring must support the Department of Vermont Health Access's Medicaid program in the context of Act 68 global budget implementation. The restructured Agency should integrate Medicaid program management with hospital sector coordination in ways that the current siloed structure does not. The Vermont Chronic Care Initiative, the Blueprint, and the Agency's broader Medicaid policy work need stronger operational integration.

Technology pillar implications. The restructuring must support the Agency's data infrastructure consolidation. Currently, data infrastructure is fragmented across departments. The restructured Agency should have a unified data architecture that supports population health management, equity reporting, program evaluation, and federal reporting requirements.

Clinical pillar implications. The restructuring must support the integration of the Blueprint, the Vermont Chronic Care Initiative, the SASH program, and the Designated Agencies into a coherent clinical infrastructure. The current departmental boundaries cut across this clinical infrastructure in ways that produce operational friction. The restructured Agency should reduce that friction.

Equity pillar implications. The restructuring must elevate the equity function within the Agency. Currently, equity work is distributed across multiple departments without a strong central function. The restructured Agency should have a stronger central equity capacity that integrates with all of the operational functions.

Operations pillar implications. The restructuring is itself operational work of substantial complexity. The work includes organizational design, position reclassification, technology system integration, contract administration redesign, financial management consolidation, and the cultural work of bringing together previously separate organizational cultures.

Element three. Sequencing decisions inside the restructuring.

The restructuring cannot be done all at once. It must be sequenced. The sequencing decisions are themselves consequential.

The book argues for a specific sequence. First, the policy and governance work, defining the structure and clarifying authority across the new Agency. Second, the data infrastructure work, building the unified data architecture that the new structure requires. Third, the financial management work, consolidating budget and financial reporting across the departments. Fourth, the operational integration work, redesigning the workflows that cross departmental boundaries. Fifth, the cultural integration work, bringing together the staff cultures of the previously separate departments.

The sequencing follows the same general logic as the broader transformation sequencing. Policy first. Technology second. Economics third. Operational integration follows. Cultural work runs throughout. The restructuring is a microcosm of the broader transformation logic.

Element four. The workforce transition plan.

The restructuring affects staff. Positions are reclassified. Reporting relationships change. New competencies are required. Some positions are consolidated. Some positions are added. The workforce transition must be managed with care.

The transition plan includes several elements. Communication, ensuring that affected staff understand what is happening and why. Reclassification, managing the personnel actions required by the structural change. Training, preparing staff for new responsibilities. Recruitment, filling positions that the new structure requires that the existing workforce cannot fill from within. Retention, ensuring that key personnel do not leave during the transition.

The political and labor dimensions of the transition are significant. Vermont state government is unionized. The transition must be managed in coordination with the relevant unions. The Agency leadership must build relationships and trust with the workforce that the success of the transition depends on.

Element five. Stakeholder alignment.

The Agency restructuring affects every constituency that the Agency serves. Hospitals. Primary care practices. Designated Agencies. Federally Qualified Health Centers. Community action agencies. Long-term care providers. Behavioral health providers. Patients and consumers. Advocacy organizations. Federal partners. Each constituency has interests in the restructuring. Each must be engaged.

The engagement is operationally substantial. Public hearings. Stakeholder meetings. Written comment processes. Coalition consultations. Federal partner briefings. Legislative testimony. The engagement is, in addition to its substantive value, the political work that builds and maintains the coalition supporting the broader transformation.

A note on the relationship between restructuring and transformation.

The Agency restructuring is not separate from Vermont's broader transformation. It is part of the transformation. The restructuring is the Operations pillar work that supports the Policy, Economics, Technology, Clinical, and Equity pillar work that Act 68 requires.

The risk of the restructuring is that the operational complexity of the restructuring itself will consume management attention that the transformation needs. The mitigation is to design the restructuring with discipline, sequence it carefully, and integrate it explicitly with the transformation timeline. The Agency must walk and chew gum at the same time. The restructuring is the chewing. The transformation is the walking. Both must continue.
