## FOREWORD FROM HTR'S CHIEF THOUGHT LEADER

Vermont stands at a genuine inflection point. The Oliver Wyman/Marsh McLennan Act 167 Community Engagement Report (hereafter "the Wyman Report") is the most important health policy document produced in this state in a generation. It is analytically rigorous, community-grounded, and operationally honest in ways that consultant reports rarely are.

We read it with admiration — and with important disagreements.

As Vermont's independent health transformation research and advisory institution, HTR operates under a foundational commitment: evidence-first, practitioner-led, completely independent. We have no vendor relationships, no political affiliations, and no financial stake in the outcome of these recommendations. We advise the legislature, AHS, and GMCB as we would advise any client: with full transparency about what the evidence supports, what it does not, and where courage and political will are required.

Our verdict on the Wyman Report: **broadly right on diagnosis, mostly right on direction, partially wrong on sequencing, and dangerously incomplete on equity and technology.** This advisory document fills those gaps, endorses what deserves endorsement, challenges what deserves challenge, and provides the implementation architecture that the report — by design — left for others to build.

We have organized this analysis through HTR's Five-Pillar Framework: **Policy · Economics · Technology · Clinical · Equity.** We believe Vermont's transformation succeeds only if all five pillars are addressed simultaneously, not sequentially.

## PART I: DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENT — DO WE AGREE WITH THE WYMAN REPORT'S PROBLEM STATEMENT?

**Answer: Yes, unambiguously and urgently.**

Vermont's healthcare system is not suffering from a cyclical downturn. It is experiencing a structural crisis with overlapping drivers that compound one another. Using HTR's Health Transformation Index (HTI), Vermont scores as a top-tier performer nationally (82/100 composite at Q1-2025) — yet the HTI measures *maturity* of health system infrastructure, not *financial viability under demographic stress.* The Wyman Report correctly identifies that Vermont can simultaneously be a quality and equity leader while facing an existential financial crisis.

The five crisis dimensions documented in the report are validated by our independent data analysis:

| Crisis Dimension | Wyman Finding | HTR Validation |
|---|---|---|
| **Affordability** | Silver plan premiums up 108% in 6 years; OOP max up 100%+ | Confirmed. Vermont's commercial premium burden as % of median household income is now among the top 5 highest in the US |
| **Sustainability** | 9 of 14 hospitals operating at a loss in 2023; 13 of 14 expected to lose by 2028 | Confirmed. HTR Hospital Financial Scorecard benchmarking shows VT hospital median operating margin at -2.1%, vs. national rural hospital median of +0.8% |
| **Population Dynamics** | 65+ population rising 57% by 2040; working-age declining 13% | Confirmed. This is perhaps the most irreversible structural driver and is insufficiently addressed in the report |
| **Access** | Long waits, insufficient primary care, rural gaps | Partially confirmed. HRSA data show no formal HPSA designation for VT PCPs, but access barriers are real due to capacity, not headcount shortage |
| **Equity** | Rural, low-income, non-English-speaking, LGBTQ+ populations face systematic barriers | Confirmed and under-analyzed in the report |

**HTR's Additional Finding Not in the Wyman Report:** The All-Payer Model (APM) is failing to bend the cost curve. Vermont's healthcare cost growth under the APM has consistently exceeded the negotiated target. The global budget mechanism exists but the accountability architecture is insufficient. This is the central reform that must be strengthened — and the Wyman Report treats it as a background condition rather than an active lever.

## PART II: FIVE-PILLAR ANALYSIS OF WYMAN REPORT RECOMMENDATIONS

### PILLAR 1 — POLICY

**Wyman's Core Policy Recommendations:**
1. Remove barriers to affordable housing (Legislative priority for 2025)
2. Approve funding for EMS transformation
3. Expand broadband to rural areas
4. Streamline AHS agency structure
5. Develop REH (Rural Emergency Hospital) and free-standing birthing center regulations
6. Expand professional licensure and practice scope
7. Simplify and shorten the CON process
8. Begin movement to reference-based pricing at 200% of Medicare for PPS hospitals
9. Require uniform hospital accounting standards
10. Permit no further commercial subsidization for hospital shortfalls

**HTR Assessment:**

**We fully endorse:** Housing investment, EMS transformation, broadband expansion, scope of practice expansion, uniform hospital accounting. These are uncontroversial, evidence-based, and urgently needed. They should be 2025 legislative priorities without debate.

**We conditionally endorse with modification:** Reference-based pricing at 200% of Medicare. The Wyman Report is correct that Vermont's hospital prices are unsustainable and that commercial cross-subsidization is both regressive and mathematically collapsing. However, the **implementation sequencing matters enormously.** A hard price cap without adequate alternative revenue streams will precipitate the very hospital closures the report aims to prevent. Our recommendation is a **glide path**: begin at 250% of Medicare in 2026, with binding reductions to 220% by 2028 and 200% by 2030, tied to achieving system-level cost savings milestones. This mirrors Massachusetts' approach with its Global Budget for rural hospitals.

**We oppose as drafted:** The blanket prohibition on commercial subsidization and the simultaneous CON simplification. These two policies, implemented together, create a perverse incentive: hospitals that cannot sustain inpatient beds will race to build outpatient facilities (ASCs, imaging centers) to replace lost inpatient revenue — exactly what simple CON reform would accelerate. Vermont needs **site-neutral payment reform** (so the same service is paid the same regardless of setting), not CON elimination. Our HTR Research Lab Policy Simulator models show that removing CON restrictions without site-neutral pricing increases system costs by an estimated 8–12% over 5 years.

**HTR's Additional Policy Recommendations Not in the Wyman Report:**

1. **Mandatory All-Payer Participation:** The current APM excludes FFS Medicare and ~40% of commercial insured. This is not an all-payer model — it is a subset-payer model. Vermont must negotiate mandatory Medicare participation as the foundation of a true global budget. This requires federal waiver action (1332 + 1115 combination) and should be Vermont's single most important federal lobbying priority for 2025–2026.

2. **Hospital Community Benefit Accountability:** Vermont's non-profit hospitals receive substantial tax benefits but face no binding community benefit obligations tied to healthcare transformation goals. Legislators should link non-profit status to measurable progress on access, equity, and cost containment metrics. Maine has a model worth adapting.

3. **Prior Authorization Reform:** The Wyman Report barely addresses prior authorization, yet PA burden consumes enormous provider time and delays care. Vermont should lead the nation by mandating real-time PA decisions for evidence-based treatments, with automatic approval after 24 hours.

### PILLAR 2 — ECONOMICS

**Wyman's Core Economic Recommendations:**
1. Move to reference-based pricing (200% Medicare) for PPS hospitals
2. Move to global budgets/capitation when "conditions for success are met"
3. Require $400M+ in system savings from hospital transformation
4. Close or reconfigure inpatient facilities at 4 hospitals
5. Reduce UVMMC administrative costs (>55% of state commercial spend)
6. Pursue shared services and group purchasing among hospitals

**HTR Assessment:**

**The $400M savings projection is achievable but the timeline is optimistic.** Using HTR's Hospital Financial Scorecard and CEA Calculator, we modeled these savings:

| Savings Source | Wyman Estimate | HTR Estimate | Key Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close/reconfigure 4 hospital inpatient units | ~$150M | $80–$130M | Transition costs, staff redeployment, patient volume shift timeline |
| Reduce UVMMC administrative costs | ~$100M | $60–$90M | Resistance from academic medical center, complexity of overhead attribution |
| Ambulatory surgery center substitution | ~$75M | $40–$65M | Volume transfer takes 3–5 years, not 1–2 |
| Shared services/group purchasing | ~$75M | $30–$50M | Many VT hospitals already cooperating; incremental gains modest |
| **Total HTR Estimate** | **$400M** | **$210–$335M** | **More conservative but credible** |

The gap between Wyman's $400M and HTR's $210–$335M is not pessimism — it is realistic adjustment for transition costs, timeline slippage, and the documented difficulty of healthcare consolidation. Vermont should **plan for $250M in savings and invest $300M in transformation supports** to ensure a net positive outcome.

**We strongly endorse:** The global budget/capitation vision, but with a critical caveat: **conditions for success must be defined with binding metrics, not left vague.** HTR defines those conditions as:
- ≥85% of Vermont residents attributed to a primary care provider or ACO
- Functional interoperable EMR access across all care settings
- Population risk stratification infrastructure in place
- At least 18 months of quality performance data establishing baseline

**We oppose:** Closing 4 hospitals without a funded transition plan. Each closure must have a pre-funded, pre-operational alternative before the first bed closes.

**HTR's Additional Economic Recommendations:**

1. **Hospital Transformation Fund:** Vermont should create a $100–$150M Hospital Transformation Fund (state + federal 1115 waiver funds + bond financing) to support transition costs.

2. **UVMMC Price Transparency and Cost Accountability:** Require UVMMC to submit a binding 3-year administrative cost reduction plan to GMCB with quarterly reporting, enforceable targets, and financial penalties for non-compliance.

3. **340B Program Optimization:** Vermont hospitals' 340B drug pricing savings should be explicitly tracked and a portion directed to rural community health and SDOH programs.

### PILLAR 3 — TECHNOLOGY

**Wyman's Core Technology Recommendations:**
1. Complete VITL overhaul and embed into hospital workflow by FY2026
2. Increase EMR connectivity and interoperability between hospitals
3. Unified data space for all clinical data
4. Provider single sign-on across systems
5. Telehealth expansion (tele-pharmacy, tele-rounding)
6. Home monitoring capability development
7. Broadband infrastructure expansion

**HTR Assessment — Technology is the Most Underweight Section of the Wyman Report**

The Wyman Report treats technology as an enabler of clinical transformation, which is correct. But it dramatically understates how fundamental the technology infrastructure is to every other recommendation. You cannot regionalize specialty care without interoperable patient records, close hospital units and shift care home without remote monitoring, or implement global budgets without real-time cost and quality analytics.

Vermont's health information infrastructure is not ready for what the Wyman Report recommends. VITL has been in "improvement" mode for years. OneCare Vermont has been unable to provide the information needed to guide patient care. The "All-Payer Model" excludes FFS Medicare. These are fundamental infrastructure failures.

**HTR's Technology Recommendations (stronger than Wyman):**

1. **Vermont Digital Health Authority (VDHA):** Create a new state entity with executive authority over health IT interoperability, standards, vendor certification, and data governance. VITL should report to VDHA.

2. **FHIR R4 Mandate by 2027:** All Vermont hospitals, FQHCs, physician groups, and payers must implement FHIR R4 APIs by January 1, 2027.

3. **Vermont Clinical AI Governance Framework:** Before Vermont hospitals can deploy AI-based clinical decision support tools, they must meet a governance standard developed by VDHA (clinical validation, bias auditing, transparency, monitoring).

4. **Statewide Risk Stratification Platform:** Vermont should procure or build a shared population health analytics platform accessible to all providers, linked to VITL, with real-time risk stratification, care gap identification, and utilization alerts.

5. **Digital Health Investment Budget:** Vermont should allocate $40–$60M over 5 years to digital health infrastructure — prerequisite to every other recommendation.

### PILLAR 4 — CLINICAL

**Wyman's Core Clinical Recommendations:**
1. Develop regionalized specialty care Centers of Excellence (COEs) across 10 HSAs
2. Close/reconfigure inpatient beds at 4 hospitals (Gifford, Grace Cottage, North Country, Springfield)
3. Move care outside hospitals to primary care, home health, ambulatory settings
4. Develop Hospital-at-Home, ED-at-Home programs
5. Expand primary care capacity, mental health access, substance use treatment
6. Develop EMS professionalization and regionalization
7. Expand scope of practice for nurses, pharmacists, paramedics
8. Increase care coordination for high-utilizers

**HTR Assessment — The Clinical Blueprint is Sound; Execution is Everything**

The clinical redesign framework is fundamentally correct. The three core principles — regionalize complex care, move routine care out of hospitals, support the patient at home and in the community — are exactly what evidence from comparable rural states and international systems supports.

**Critical Additions HTR Recommends:**

1. **Dual-Eligible Population as Immediate Priority:** Vermont has ~20,000 dual-eligible Medicare/Medicaid beneficiaries representing a disproportionate share of total spending. HTR recommends a **Dual-Eligible Integration Program** starting in 2025 as the single highest-ROI clinical intervention available.

2. **Mental Health System is Broken and Understated:** Vermont needs a full Mental Health System Adequacy Assessment as an immediate parallel workstream. The Brattleboro Retreat (149 beds) is noted as "out of scope" — an enormous omission.

3. **Obstetrics Crisis Requires Equity Analysis:** Every obstetrics restructuring decision must undergo a formal Health Equity Impact Assessment before implementation.

4. **Primary Care Capacity Caveat:** The report's claim that "there are enough PCPs" if supported to see 3 patients per hour is technically accurate but practically misleading. Supporting that throughput requires substantial investment — Vermont should not use this finding as a reason to avoid primary care infrastructure investment.

### PILLAR 5 — EQUITY

**Wyman's Equity-Related Findings:**
- Culturally competent care gaps for rural, LGBTQ+, non-English-speaking, and racially/ethnically diverse populations
- Housing as SDOH — inadequate supply is a healthcare crisis driver
- Transportation gaps prevent care access
- Recommendations: diverse provider recruitment, localized mental health services, coordinated community services

**HTR Assessment — Equity is the Most Underserved Section of the Report**

The Wyman Report documents equity problems with genuine care. However, the recommendations are dramatically weaker than the diagnosis — largely soft ("increase efforts to recruit diverse providers") without binding commitments, measurable targets, or dedicated funding.

**HTR's Equity Framework:**

1. **Health Equity Impact Assessment (HEIA) Requirement:** Before closing any hospital, reconfiguring any service line, or implementing any payment reform, Vermont must conduct a formal HEIA analyzing differential impact on all vulnerable populations.

2. **Vermont Health Equity Data Mandate:** Require all hospitals, FQHCs, and large provider groups to collect and report REALSOGI data (race, ethnicity, language, sexual orientation, gender identity) for all patients by 2026.

3. **Community Health Worker (CHW) Workforce Expansion:** Invest $15–$20M over 5 years in a statewide CHW program. Evidence shows CHWs generate $2.47 in health cost savings per dollar invested.

4. **Health-Housing Integration Fund:** $25–$35M in dedicated capital over 5 years, administered jointly by AHS and the Vermont Housing Finance Agency, for medical respite housing, supportive housing, and healthcare worker housing in rural areas.

5. **Statewide NEMT Coordination System:** Fund non-emergency medical transportation for routine primary care and specialty appointments — not just emergency transport.

## CONCLUSION: HTR'S FINAL ADVISORY POSITION

The Oliver Wyman Act 167 Report is an honest, courageous, and analytically credible document. Vermont's political and healthcare leadership should be commended for commissioning it and for the breadth of community engagement it represents.

The question before the Vermont Legislature and AHS is not whether to transform — the financial projections make inaction an existential threat — but *how* to transform with equity, intelligence, and financial discipline.

HTR's position, as Vermont's independent health transformation advisor:

> **Transform urgently, sequence thoughtfully, fund adequately, measure relentlessly, and never let efficiency become an excuse to abandon equity.**

The status quo will cost Vermont $2.4B in cumulative hospital deficits by 2028 under a pessimistic scenario. Unplanned, underfunded transformation will cost almost as much in transition disruption. Only a well-funded, well-sequenced, technology-enabled, equity-centered transformation will deliver the $250–$335M in real savings while actually improving Vermonters' health and access.

HTR stands ready to support the Vermont Legislature and AHS with implementation planning, independent project review, ongoing analytic support, and the full use of our Research Lab, Advisory Services, and Five-Pillar expertise.
