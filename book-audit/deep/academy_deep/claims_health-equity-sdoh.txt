## structural-racism-health
25	A mortgage map drawn in 1937, a school funded by local property taxes, a highway routed through a particular neighborhood — none of these requires a bigot in the room to produce racially patterned harm decades later.
26	In 1933, during the Great Depression, Congress created the Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) to refinance mortgages and stem foreclosures.
27	Beginning in 1935, as part of its City Survey project (which ran through 1940), the HOLC produced color-coded "residential security maps" of more than 200 cities, grading neighborhoods for lending risk: A ("Best," outlined green), B ("Still Desirable," blue), C ("Declining," yellow), and D ("Hazardous," outlined red).
28	The remarkable and disturbing finding of modern research is how well those 1930s maps still predict health today, more than 80 years later.
29	A national NCRC analysis found life expectancy roughly 3.6 years lower on average in historically redlined (grade D) communities versus the highest-graded (grade A) communities.
30	Asthma is a striking marker: in eight California cities, asthma emergency department visit rates were about 2.4 times higher in grade-D than in grade-A tracts (Nardone et al., Lancet Planetary Health, 2020), and a 2024 multi-city birth-cohort study tied redlining to higher childhood asthma risk through neighborhood poverty.

## food-insecurity-clinical-interventions
174	According to the USDA, in 2023 about 13.5% of US households (18.0 million households) experienced food insecurity at some point during the year, and roughly 47 million people lived in food-insecure households, including nearly 14 million children.
175	Both figures rose from 2022 and reached their highest levels in years.
176	In 2023, ~47 million Americans — including nearly 14 million children — lived in food-insecure households.
177	The Hunger Vital Sign, a validated two-item tool, asks whether in the past 12 months the household worried food would run out before there was money to buy more, and whether food bought did not last.
178	Developed by researchers at Children's HealthWatch and validated against the longer USDA food-security module, the Hunger Vital Sign is a two-question screen: (1) "Within the past 12 months we worried whether our food would run out before we got money to buy more," and (2) "Within the past 12 months the food we bought just didn't last and we didn't have money to get more."
179	Its brevity has made it one of the most widely adopted social-needs screens in clinical settings, including pediatrics.
180	In a widely cited study by Berkowitz and colleagues, receiving medically tailored meals was associated with a 49% reduction in inpatient admissions, 72% fewer skilled-nursing-facility admissions, and about 16% lower total health care costs compared with similar patients who did not receive them.
181	A study published in JAMA Network Open by researchers at Tufts University's Friedman School estimated that a nationwide medically tailored meal program for eligible patients could avert roughly 1.6 million hospitalizations and save insurers on the order of $13.6 billion annually.
182	Several evaluations have found meaningful improvements: a large Tufts-led analysis reported modest A1c reductions (around 0.3 percentage points) along with reduced food insecurity and improved diet, and some physician-supported programs have reported larger A1c drops of roughly a full point.

## pulse-oximetry-racial-bias
255	The pulse oximeter, one of the most ubiquitous devices in all of medicine, is the clearest and most consequential example.
256	A pulse oximeter estimates blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) by shining light through tissue and measuring how much is absorbed.
257	The Landmark Evidence: Sjoding et al., 2020
258	Although clinicians had noted the discrepancy for years, the study that crystallized the issue was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in December 2020 by Michael Sjoding and colleagues at the University of Michigan.
259	They compared pulse oximeter readings (SpO2) against the gold-standard arterial blood gas measurement (SaO2) in two large patient cohorts, looking specifically for "occult hypoxemia."
260	Occult hypoxemia is the dangerous scenario this bias produces: a true arterial oxygen saturation below 88% — clinically low — despite a pulse oximeter reading in the reassuring 92-96% range.
261	In the multicenter cohort, occult hypoxemia occurred in 17.0% of Black patients but only 6.2% of white patients — Black patients had nearly three times the rate of dangerously low oxygen that the pulse oximeter failed to detect.
262	The mechanism was attributed to greater melanin causing falsely elevated SpO2 readings.
263	The timing made it urgent: the study landed during the COVID-19 pandemic, when oxygen saturation was the central triage variable for a disease that was already killing Black Americans at disproportionate rates.

## cross-sector-partnerships
377	The most ambitious federal test of cross-sector partnership was the CMS Accountable Health Communities (AHC) Model, which ran from 2017 to 2023.
378	The model was built around "bridge organizations" — 32 diverse entities, including community-based organizations, hospitals, health systems, payers, and health information exchanges, that coordinated screening and referral across sectors in their communities.
379	Over its life, AHC organizations screened more than one million beneficiaries — a genuinely large-scale test.
380	In the assistance track, the evaluation found lower total expenditures for beneficiaries offered navigation and fewer Medicaid inpatient admissions and unplanned readmissions; CMS’s final evaluation credited the model with more than $200 million in net savings, almost 80% of it among Medicaid-only beneficiaries.
381	AHC was CMS's flagship cross-sector demonstration: screen Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries for five core social needs and connect them to community resources through 32 "bridge organizations." It screened over 1.1 million beneficiaries.
382	In the randomized assistance track, beneficiaries who received navigation showed lower total expenditures and fewer unplanned readmissions — more than $200 million in net savings overall, per CMS’s final evaluation.
383	CMS Accountable Health Communities Model (2017-2023)
384	Partnerships need money, and one of the most significant funding levers is the community benefit obligation that nonprofit hospitals carry.
385	Most US hospitals are tax-exempt 501(c)(3) organizations, and in exchange for that exemption they are required to provide community benefit.
386	Under Section 9007 of the ACA (codified as Internal Revenue Code Section 501(r)), every tax-exempt hospital must conduct a Community Health Needs Assessment (CHNA) at least once every three years, with input from people representing the broad interests of the community, including public health expertise — and must adopt an implementation strategy describing how it will address the priority needs identified.
387	Hospitals report their community benefit spending and compliance on IRS Form 990, Schedule H.
388	The requirements have teeth: a hospital that fails to conduct its triennial CHNA can face a $50,000 excise tax.

## organizational-equity-transformation
403	The most influential roadmap for organizational transformation is the Institute for Healthcare Improvement's 2016 white paper, "Achieving Health Equity: A Guide for Health Care Organizations." It lays out a framework of five interdependent components, and its core argument is that meaningful progress requires work across all five — not cherry-picking the comfortable ones.
404	The Joint Commission — the body that accredits most US hospitals — issued new Leadership standards on health equity effective January 1, 2023, and then elevated health equity to a National Patient Safety Goal effective July 1, 2023.
405	The standard (LD.04.03.08) was deliberately placed in the Leadership chapter, with the Joint Commission explaining that "success demands leadership" and that achieving equity requires commitment from the C-suite and the board.
406	Effective January 1, 2023, the Joint Commission introduced a Leadership standard requiring accredited hospitals to treat health care disparities as a quality and safety priority; effective July 1, 2023, it elevated this to a National Patient Safety Goal.
407	The Joint Commission Health Care Equity Standard (LD.04.03.08)
408	It is reinforced every time leadership funds an equity intervention over a competing priority, and undermined every time equity is the first thing cut under pressure.
