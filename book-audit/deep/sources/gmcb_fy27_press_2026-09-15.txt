Green Mountain Care Board Approves FY27 Hospital Budgets 
Decisions improve affordability for Vermonters by reducing high hospital commercial prices 
Montpelier, VT — The Green Mountain Care Board (GMCB) completed its FY27 hospital budget 
review, establishing budgets for Vermont’s 14 community hospitals following months of review, public 
hearings and deliberations. 
Collectively, the FY27 decisions result in a 2.9% reduction in commercial reimbursement rates 
statewide and a 1.2% reduction in commercial net patient revenue (NPR). Many Vermont hospitals have 
high commercial prices, and GMCB’s FY27 decisions reflect the need to ensure Vermonters have access 
to affordable healthcare.   
The annual hospital budget review is one of the Board’s primary tools for slowing the growth of 
healthcare spending in Vermont. Each year, Vermont’s community hospitals submit proposed budgets to 
the Board, which considers hospital finances, local healthcare needs and resources, utilization, quality, 
workforce and other factors before establishing each hospital’s budget. 
The FY27 process began with the Board establishing budget guidance in March. Hospitals submitted 
proposed budgets by July 1 and presented their requests during public hearings in August. The Board 
began public deliberations on September 2 and completed its decisions September 14. 
“Vermont patients do not have the resources needed to balance the financial requirements of our hospital 
system and system-wide change is needed.  GMCB’s decisions reflect Vermonters’ rights to an 
affordable hospital system and continue the State’s push toward equitable and fair hospital pricing,” said 
GMCB Chair Owen Foster. “Vermont has a tremendous opportunity to sustain our critical hospital 
system, preserve essential services, and improve affordability through ongoing hospital transformation 
efforts, distribution and prudent use of Rural Health Transformation Program funds, and through 
insurers and hospitals working together to identify—and adequately reimburse—essential community 
services.”

FY27 Statewide Results 
For FY27, the Board established approximately $3.8 billion in total net patient revenue across Vermont's 
14 community hospitals, an increase of 1.6% over FY26 approved budgets. 
Within that total: 
• Commercial NPR: $1.937 billion — 1.2% decrease statewide  
• Commercial reimbursement rates: 2.9% decrease statewide  
 
Net patient revenue is the revenue hospitals receive for providing patient care after contractual 
allowances, uncompensated care and other deductions from gross charges. Commercial NPR represents 
the portion associated with patients covered by commercial insurance. 
New Focus on Total Commercial Spending 
FY27 represents an important evolution in the Board's hospital budget review. In addition to regulating 
overall hospital net patient revenue and reimbursement rates, the Board established limits on commercial 
net patient revenue. 
This approach recognizes that healthcare spending is driven by more than price alone and encourages 
hospitals to focus on population health and to ensure patients receive the right care, at the right place, at 
the right time.  
 
The Board’s FY27 guidance generally called for a 1% reduction in commercial NPR. In establishing 
individual hospital budgets, the Board considered that guidance alongside each hospital’s circumstances, 
including financial condition, commercial prices, utilization, access needs, workforce, capital needs and 
the communities served. 
 
Increased Transparency Around Support for Medical Education 
As part of the FY27 budget process, the Board also established a working group to provide greater 
transparency and oversight into the University of Vermont Medical Center’s financial support of the 
Larner College of Medicine. 
 
The hospital budget review identified more than $80 million annually in commercial financial support 
from UVMMC to the Larner College of Medicine. Because those costs are supported through 
commercial hospital revenues, they contribute to the healthcare costs ultimately borne by commercially 
insured Vermonters and employers. The working group will examine this financial relationship and 
develop recommendations aimed at ensuring Vermonters receive the maximum benefit from Larner as a 
critical institution and resource for the state. 
 
The working group will include representatives from UVM, Larner, the Office of the Health Care 
Advocate, GMCB and other healthcare and community stakeholders. Its work will be conducted through

Connection to Health Insurance Affordability 
Hospital spending is a significant component of overall healthcare spending and contributes to the 
premiums paid by Vermont individuals, families and employers 
 
The aggregate FY27 hospital budget decisions are closely aligned with the hospital cost assumptions 
incorporated into the Board’s recently completed 2027 individual and small group health insurance rate 
review. Together, the two regulatory processes reflect the Board’s continued focus on addressing the 
underlying cost of healthcare and improving affordability for Vermonters. 
 
FY27 Hospital Budget Decisions 
 
The Board’s decisions establish maximum revenue and reimbursement rate levels for FY27. Individual 
hospital budget orders will include standard budget conditions as well as hospital-specific conditions 
where applicable. 
The Board will issue FY27 budget orders to hospitals by October 1, when the new hospital fiscal year 
begins, and will continue to monitor hospital financial performance throughout the year. 
Hospital budget submissions, hearing and deliberation materials, final budget orders and additional 
information about the FY27 Hospital Budget Review will be available on the Green Mountain Care 
Board website.

About: The Green Mountain Care Board (GMCB) is a 5-member, independent Board with a vision of a sustainable and 
equitable healthcare system that promotes better health outcomes for Vermonters. GMCB was created in 2011 with an 
ambitious mission to drive system-wide improvements in access, affordability, and quality of healthcare to improve the health 
of Vermonters. Through public meetings, GMCB ensures a transparent approach to healthcare regulation and a voice for 
stakeholders, including healthcare organizations, clinicians, and members of the public. With a holistic, data driven 
approach, GMCB carries out its regulatory duties, supports innovation in healthcare delivery and payment reform, and 
serves as an important resource for independent, transparent analyses of Vermont’s healthcare system performance.
