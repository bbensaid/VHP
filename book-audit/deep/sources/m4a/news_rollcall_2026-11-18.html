Sanders: ‘Medicare for All’ will be HELP priority

Vermont senator also says he’s ‘all in’ on extension of Affordable Care Act enhanced subsidies

Sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vt., holds a news conference in the Capitol on Nov. 10. (Bill Clark/CQ Roll Call)

By Hannah Campbell
Posted November 18, 2026 at 7:03pm

Saying that “Medicare for All” is no longer “a radical idea,” Sen. Bernie Sanders said Wednesday that he would prioritize passing the universal health care plan if he assumes the chairmanship of the Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee next year as expected.

Democrats could bring it about through the filibuster-proof budget reconciliation process, the Vermont independent and avowed democratic socialist told reporters. And the idea could be buoyed by an influx of progressive lawmakers elected in the midterms.

Sanders said Medicare for All is gaining support from voters across the country who are looking to overhaul the American health care system as costs have soared. Democrats on the committee will move the legislation “as aggressively as we can, as rapidly as we can,” he said.

His version, introduced in April and referred to the Senate Finance Committee, would implement the universal health care system over four years. In the first year, the the age of Medicare eligibility would drop to 55 from 65, while coverage would expand to include dental, hearing and vision care. The bill would also provide coverage for eligible children during the first year.

The age of eligibility would fall another 10 years in each of the following years until the fourth year, when coverage would be extended to all Americans.

“If there has ever been a time in the modern history of our country . . . to address the crises facing working families, this is that time,” Sanders said, noting that Democrats will have a 56-44 majority when the 120th Congress begins in January, the largest margin for either party in 16 years.

Sen. Tammy Baldwin, D-Wis., along with nine other Senate Democrats, last week offered an alternative Medicare expansion plan, which would lower the eligibility to those ages 50 and up.

While who will be leading Senate Democrats is still an open question — Chris Van Hollen of Maryland is challenging Charles E. Schumer of New York, who has been the top Senate Democrat since 2017 — Sanders said he is confident the Medicare for All bill will get to the Senate floor, “no matter who the [Senate majority] leader is.” Sanders previously expressed opposition to Schumer leading the conference.

Asked about efforts to extend the Affordable Care Act enhanced subsidies, Sanders responded he’s “all in.” However, he added “the American people and elected officials understand the current health care system is broken.”

Subsidies that help enrollees pay for health care premiums on the ACA exchanges were expanded during the COVID-19 pandemic, but these enhanced credits expired at the end of last year, raising costs for many who relied on them to help defray costs.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, nominated on Wednesday to be speaker next year, previously outlined broader health care priorities such as extending the ACA tax credits and reversing Medicaid cuts enacted in Republicans' reconciliation package last year. When asked Tuesday how far Democrats want to lower the Medicare eligibility age, Jeffries responded, “that’s all part of an ongoing conversation.”

In addition to expanding Medicare coverage, Sanders reiterated his push to lower prescription drug prices. The Vermont lawmaker previously introduced legislation aimed at reducing the cost of brand name prescription drugs by linking domestic prices to international price benchmarks.

To enact some of the agenda, Sanders may have to court Republicans.

Sen. Rand Paul, R-Ky., said last week that he plans to serve as the top Republican on the HELP committee next year. Sanders said Wednesday that his likely GOP counterpart “has shown an enormous amount of courage in taking on Republican leadership year after year.”

Sanders added that although Paul may have a different viewpoint from himself on committee issues, he looks forward to finding common ground.
