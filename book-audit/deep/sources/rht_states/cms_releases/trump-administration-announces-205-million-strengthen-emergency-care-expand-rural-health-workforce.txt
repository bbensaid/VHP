This investment will improve access to emergency medical services, grow the rural healthcare workforce, and modernize healthcare technology statewide. Wyoming has committed its full $205 million in funding toward improving rural healthcare in communities across the state. This transformational funding includes $91.8 million to improve access to emergency medical care, including labor and delivery, EMS, and trauma services, while supporting the long-term sustainability of critical access to hospitals and rural ambulance services. An additional $56.3 million will support education and training to grow the pipeline of nurses, EMTs, behavioral health workers, and physicians. Another $16.9 million will support technology that will help bring care closer to home, manage chronic disease, and connect patients to clinical specialists. Another $38.6 million will support the Make Wyoming Healthy Again initiative, expanding access to primary and behavioral healthcare and supporting healthy diet and exercise programs. The Rural Health Transformation Program is expanding access to quality healthcare, regardless of a patient's ZIP code, while equipping first responders with the lifesaving resources they need when every second counts. This funding will make critical investments to strengthen rural healthcare, expand access to lifesaving services, and bring modern technology to communities across Wyoming. “President Trump understands that every American deserves high-quality healthcare, no matter where they live,” said Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr. “We are putting resources directly into rural communities to strengthen the health workforce, modernize infrastructure, expand access, and bring care closer to home. We are giving states the flexibility to build solutions around the people they serve, while demanding accountability for results. That is how we strengthen rural health, improve the lives of patients and families, and Make America Healthy Again.” “President Trump made a historic commitment to rural America, and these investments are turning that commitment into better care for patients in communities across Wyoming,” said Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz. “This is what rural health transformation should look like: federal investment that is putting patients first, driving lasting improvements and expanding healthcare for the hardworking Americans who call these communities home.” Today’s investment was made possible through the Working Families Tax Cuts Act — passed by Republicans in Congress and unanimously opposed by Democrats — delivering a historic $50 billion investment to help states transform the way healthcare is delivered in America. “Wyoming is a unique state with far flung communities a great distance apart. We need flexibility and sustainability that meets healthcare needs across the State. The Rural Health Transformation Project equips Wyoming to continue to find solutions for our rural and frontier communities,” Gov. Mark Gordon said. “I want to thank President Trump, Wyoming’s United States Senator John Barrasso and Administrator Oz for their leadership in bringing this program to fruition.” “As a doctor who practiced medicine in Wyoming for 24 years, I know how important affordable, reliable, and quality local care is for our rural communities. That’s why I fought last year to create the Rural Health Transformation Program,” said Sen. John Barrasso. “Wyoming is using this year’s $205 million investment to strengthen and expand access to care in every corner of our state. We are supporting our rural hospitals, clinics, and emergency medicine services. We are investing in training and recruiting highly skilled doctors, nurses, and other healthcare professionals. We are embracing new technologies and bringing quality care closer to home. This is just the beginning. More funding will follow over the next four years. I’m confident this historic investment in rural healthcare will improve care for families across Wyoming.” “This investment of more than $200 million in Wyoming’s rural health systems is a major step toward keeping emergency, primary, and behavioral care close to home. I was proud to support the Working Families Tax Cuts Act, and I want to thank Senator Barrasso for championing the Rural Health Transformation Program, which delivers funding for rural hospitals, a stronger workforce, and modern, technology-enabled care. I’m glad to see this funding released, and I know it will make a real difference for Wyoming,” said Sen. Cynthia Lummis. “The Working Families Tax Cuts law delivers game-changing investments in health care for rural residents routinely overlooked by Washington reforms. This $205 million injected into rural communities with local autonomy on how funds are spent is saving lives by giving hard-to-reach places the resources they need to receive the care and treatment their health deserves in the world’s most prosperous nation. The Rural Health Transformation Program will go down as one of the most truly transformative elements of this administration’s signature legislative accomplishment,” said Rep. Harriet Hageman. Today’s announcement highlights the overall funding amount awarded to Wyoming for fiscal year 2026. Key initiatives the overall RHTP funding will focus on include: Building the rural healthcare workforce: Supporting recruitment, training, and retention strategies to strengthen the pipeline of healthcare professionals serving rural communities. Strengthening rural facilities and access: Supporting partnerships, operational improvements, and care-delivery changes that help rural providers maintain essential services and improve access. Advancing innovative care and payment models: Supporting new approaches to delivery and financing designed to improve quality, strengthen primary care, and support sustainable rural healthcare. Expanding community-based and preventive care: Investing in school-based care, mobile services, and other community strategies that improve prevention, address chronic disease, and expand access to care. Modernizing rural healthcare technology: Expanding remote care and data-driven tools to improve coordination and extend healthcare services to rural communities. The Trump Administration is delivering on its commitment to strengthen rural America by investing in solutions that modernize technology to promote health and well-being for rural communities. Led by CMS, the RHTP is moving beyond the failed one-size-fits-all approach and giving rural communities the tools to build stronger, more innovative, and more sustainable health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes for the Americans they serve.
