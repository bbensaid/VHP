This investment will help rural providers deliver and coordinate care locally through regional care networks, telehealth, workforce development, and upgraded healthcare technology. California has committed its full fiscal year 2026 funding — more than $233 million — to improve healthcare in rural communities across the state. This transformational investment will help rural Californians receive more primary, maternal, specialty, and behavioral healthcare in their communities. Regional care networks and telehealth will connect patients with providers and help coordinate care, while partnerships with midwives, doulas, and community health workers will support maternal and primary care. Investments will also support education and training pathways to grow the rural healthcare workforce, improve technology, and modernize infrastructure — helping providers deliver expanded care locally. The Rural Health Transformation Program is expanding access to quality healthcare, regardless of a patient’s ZIP code, while equipping first responders with the lifesaving resources they need when every second counts. This funding will make critical investments to strengthen rural healthcare, expand access to lifesaving services, and bring modern technology to communities across California. “President Trump understands that every American deserves high-quality healthcare, no matter where they live,” said Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr. “We are putting resources directly into rural communities to strengthen the health workforce, modernize infrastructure, expand access, and bring care closer to home. We are giving states the flexibility to build solutions around the people they serve, while demanding accountability for results. For too long, rural communities have been asked to do more with less. It’s time we changed that, and this funding is a good start.” “Delivering health care across the rural counties in my district has always presented unique challenges,” said Rep. I fully support the ongoing efforts by the Trump Administration and CMS to provide health care solutions that lower costs and increase access to care across the country, including rural communities,” said Rep. Ken Calvert. Today’s announcement highlights the overall funding amount awarded to California for fiscal year 2026. Key initiatives the overall RHTP funding will focus on include: Building the rural healthcare workforce: Supporting recruitment, training, and retention strategies to strengthen the pipeline of healthcare professionals serving rural communities. Strengthening rural facilities and access: Supporting partnerships, operational improvements, and care-delivery changes that help rural providers maintain essential services and improve access. Advancing innovative care and payment models: Supporting new approaches to delivery and financing designed to improve quality, strengthen primary care, and support sustainable rural healthcare. Expanding community-based and preventive care: Investing in school-based care, mobile services, and other community strategies that improve prevention, address chronic disease, and expand access to care. Modernizing rural healthcare technology: Expanding remote care and data-driven tools to improve coordination and extend healthcare services to rural communities. The Trump Administration is delivering on its commitment to strengthen rural America by investing in solutions that modernize technology to promote health and well-being for rural communities. Led by CMS, the RHTP is moving beyond the failed one-size-fits-all approach and giving rural communities the tools to build stronger, more innovative, and more sustainable health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes for the Americans they serve.
