These investments will support locally driven solutions across eight regions of the state including removing barriers to technology, transportation, and health education. They will provide nutritional support and counseling with community-specific pilot programs that will address the impact food and nutrition have on health and healthcare. This funding will also help address prevention and management of chronic conditions such as diabetes and obesity. This investment will strengthen maternal and infant health services, expand access to primary and behavioral health care, and provide critical mental health and crisis response resources. It will also help build the next generation of the rural health workforce through expanded recruitment, training and retention efforts, while creating a coordinated transportation network to help patients get to the care they need. “President Trump understands that every American deserves high-quality healthcare, no matter where they live,” said Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. These investments mark a major milestone in Indiana’s long-term effort to support healthier families, expand access to essential services and build sustainable rural healthcare systems. Today’s announcement is just one part of the larger overall funding amount awarded to Indiana for fiscal year 2026. Projects supported by this funding include: Improving Maternal and Infant Health: Providing prenatal and postpartum navigation, maternal fetal medicine access, infant support, prenatal mental health support and a rural-focused OB residency. Increasing Access to Primary Care, Behavioral Healthcare, and Crisis Response: Providing new access points by expanding clinical capacity, adding primary care and behavioral health access points by launching mobile medical and dental services, and transforming behavioral health and crisis response through Emergency Department embedded clinicians and standardized regional crisis service messaging. Removing barriers to technology, healthy food, and health education. Growing the Rural Health Workforce: Building a regional rural health workforce pipeline with expanded recruitment, clinical training pathways, retention strategies and high-need positions. Improving Rural Transportation and Access to Care: Developing a coordinated reginal transportation network linking hospitals, EMS, aging-services providers and local transit to reduce missed appointments and improve access to care. Chronic Disease Prevention and Management: Evidence‑based, outcomes‑driven interventions for conditions like diabetes, hypertension, obesity and related risks Food and Nutrition Support: Incorporating food and nutrition pilots into rural communities’ healthcare availability, including a pilot initiative to deliver locally sourced, Hoosier-grown food for medically tailored meals and fresh produce prescriptions paired with telehealth-based nutrition therapy. Other initiatives include integrating nutritional support into maternal health programs. The Trump Administration is delivering on its commitment to strengthen rural America by investing in solutions that transform healthcare access for rural communities. Led by CMS, the RHTP is moving beyond the failed one-size-fits-all approach and giving rural communities the tools to build stronger, more innovative, and more sustainable health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes for the Americans they serve.
