The investment will support 34 grants to healthcare providers and institutions serving rural communities across Alabama. Projects will equip at least 20 rural hospitals with emergency labor and delivery resources; expand maternal-fetal medicine through telehealth and telerobotic ultrasound; strengthen trauma and stroke coordination and EMS treat-in-place services; expand mobile and community-based cancer screening; and build statewide healthcare workforce pipelines through Alabama's schools and community colleges. The Rural Health Transformation Program is expanding access to quality healthcare, regardless of a patient’s zip code, so rural Alabama mothers can get critical care closer to home, emergency teams can respond when every second counts, and more patients can detect cancer earlier, when treatment can be most effective. This funding will strengthen Alabama’s healthcare workforce, expand access to maternal, emergency, and preventive care, and bring modern technology and innovative services closer to patients across the state. “President Trump understands that every American deserves high-quality healthcare, no matter where they live,” said Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Key initiatives in Alabama supported by RHTP funding include: Bringing maternal care closer to home: Connecting rural facilities with maternal-fetal medicine specialists through digital tools and telerobotic ultrasound. Expanding emergency labor and delivery resources to help stabilize mothers and newborns before transfer. Building Alabama’s rural healthcare workforce: Expanding training and recruitment pipelines for physicians, nurses, EMS professionals, midwives, dental professionals, and other healthcare workers through remote learning, clinical partnerships, provider incentives, hands-on training, and expanded Graduate Medical Education opportunities. Detecting cancer earlier in rural communities: Expanding early cancer detection using Alabama’s Operation Wipe Out model. Mobilizing local organizations and cross-sector partnerships to bring preventive screenings into rural communities and reach more residents efficiently. Strengthening emergency care and EMS: Expanding hospital-referral services that help EMS route patients to appropriate facilities for trauma, stroke, and other complex conditions, while supporting treat-in-place models when hospital transport is not necessary. Modernizing rural healthcare and expanding access: Investing in electronic health records, IT and cybersecurity, telehealth, behavioral healthcare, and coordinated rural care networks to better connect rural providers and patients with services. The Trump Administration is delivering on its commitment to strengthen rural America by investing in solutions that modernize, transform, and expand healthcare access for rural communities nationwide. Led by CMS, the RHTP is moving beyond the failed one-size-fits-all approach and giving rural communities the tools to build stronger, more innovative, and more sustainable health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes for the Americans they serve.
