Supporting 54 projects across rural communities in Arkansas, $27.4 of this funding, will support 30 projects focused on community-based nutrition, physical activity, preventive care, and chronic disease management. An additional $27.2 million will support 24 projects focused on recruiting, training, and retaining the rural healthcare workforce. Project outlines include mobile preventive screenings; new rural residency and clinical training programs; and expanded EMT, nursing, maternal health, and behavioral health workforce development. These investments build on the Administration’s $149.3 million previously announced to expand telehealth and specialty care, modernize rural healthcare technology and infrastructure, and strengthen emergency and preventive services across Arkansas “President Trump understands that every American deserves high-quality healthcare, no matter where they live,” said Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr. “We are putting resources directly into rural communities to strengthen the health workforce, modernize infrastructure, expand access, and bring care closer to home. We are giving states the flexibility to build solutions around the people they serve, while demanding accountability for results. By supporting initiatives focused on preventing and managing chronic diseases as well as bolstering the healthcare workforce, this funding will help address the unique challenges facing rural communities. I’m pleased to see these resources going toward improving the health and well-being of Arkansans and ensuring our rural communities are better positioned to provide care and treatment for years to come,” said Sen. Today’s announcement, one part of the larger overall funding amount awarded to Arkansas for fiscal year 2026, completes Arkansas’s initial Year 1 award cycle. These investments are expected to reach communities across all 75 Arkansas counties. Key initiatives the overall RHTP funding will focus on include: Improving prevention and chronic disease management: Expanding physical activity, preventive screenings, nutrition-focused programs, and community-based strategies to address heart disease, diabetes, hypertension, obesity, and other chronic conditions. Building the rural healthcare workforce: Expanding residency programs, professional training opportunities, recruitment, and retention strategies to strengthen the rural workforce. Expanding access and care coordination: Strengthening partnerships among rural hospitals and clinics and expanding specialty care, telehealth, and coordinated care. Advancing technology-enabled care and emergency response: Expanding telehealth, remote monitoring, home health technology, and emergency-response infrastructure to improve access and support patients with chronic conditions. The Trump Administration is delivering on its commitment to strengthen rural America by investing in solutions that modernize, transform and expand healthcare access for rural communities nationwide. Led by CMS, the RHTP is moving beyond the failed one-size-fits-all approach and giving rural communities the tools to build stronger, more innovative, and more sustainable health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes for the Americans they serve.
