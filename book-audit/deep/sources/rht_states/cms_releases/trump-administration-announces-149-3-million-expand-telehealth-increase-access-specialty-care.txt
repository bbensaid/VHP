This funding will make critical investments to strengthen rural healthcare, expand access to lifesaving services, and bring modern technology to communities across Arkansas. The administration’s investment will help upgrade ambulances, expand telehealth and patient-monitoring capabilities, upgrade rural hospitals and clinics with advanced imaging and emergency teleconsultation technology, and enhance training for rural health providers. Investments will also help hospitals maintain critical services during power outages and disasters, and expand specialty and preventive care across rural clinics. Together, these investments are modernizing rural healthcare, expanding the healthcare workforce, and ensuring Arkansans have access to the high-quality care they deserve. “President Trump understands that every American deserves high-quality healthcare, no matter where they live,” said Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr. “We are putting resources directly into rural communities to strengthen the health workforce, modernize infrastructure, expand access, and bring care closer to home. Today’s announcement is just one part of the larger overall funding amount awarded to Arkansas for fiscal year 2026. Examples of projects supported by this funding include: $10 million to replace ambulances and add telehealth communications, connectivity and patient-monitoring equipment, data sharing, training, and a Patient Navigation Hub. $6,340,043 to upgrade rural hospitals and clinics with specialty telehealth, AI-enabled SMART rooms, telemetry and imaging equipment, digital integration, and emergency teleconsultation capability. $2,337,402 to provide telehealth assessments, infrastructure upgrades, access points, and digital health training through seven rural partners. $5 million to purchase and install emergency generators, integrate electrical systems, and conduct testing and preparedness activities so participating rural hospitals can maintain critical services during outages and disasters. $12 million to expand specialty and preventive care, modernize infrastructure, create a regional network intended to serve 2,400 rural residents by Year 5, expand services in 10 clinics, and increase provider data sharing. $1,228,624 to create a telehealth hearing-care network that provides screening, specialist triage, diagnosis, and treatment through community sites and rural clinics. The Trump Administration is delivering on its commitment to strengthen rural America by investing in solutions that modernize technology to promote health and well-being for rural communities. Led by CMS, the RHTP is moving beyond the failed one-size-fits-all approach and giving rural communities the tools to build stronger, more innovative, and more sustainable health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes for the Americans they serve.
