This investment will support hospital infrastructure improvements, expand behavioral and maternal healthcare access, and strengthen the rural emergency medical services workforce. The announcement includes approximately $35 million to support 20 rural hospital projects — covering facility improvements, building system upgrades, new diagnostic equipment, and renovated critical care and treatment spaces. It also includes up to $6.4 million to connect rural providers with behavioral health and perinatal specialists, including rapid psychiatric consultations, often within 30 minutes, for children and families, along with referral services, provider training, and additional support for pregnant and postpartum patients. And up to $4.2 million will grow Missouri's rural EMS workforce, with 437 individuals selected to begin funded EMT and paramedic training and incentives for rural agencies to hire and retain certified staff. These investments build on the Administration’s more than $7 million in funding previously announced to strengthen Missouri’s rural maternal health workforce through expanded doula training and certification and to expand mobile healthcare and community paramedicine services across rural communities. “President Trump understands that every American deserves high-quality healthcare, no matter where they live,” said Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Today’s announcement is one part of the larger overall funding amount awarded to Missouri for fiscal year 2026. Missouri's broader RHTP strategy is designed to strengthen coordination and build a more sustainable rural healthcare system. Key initiatives the overall RHTP funding will focus on include: Strengthening local and regional care networks: Supporting local community hubs and regional networks to improve care coordination and better connect rural Missourians with healthcare providers and services. Building the rural healthcare workforce: Supporting recruitment, training, clinical education, and retention strategies to strengthen the pipeline of healthcare professionals serving rural communities. Advancing technology and data: Building shared digital infrastructure and improving interoperability and data sharing to better connect rural providers and support coordinated care. Transforming rural care delivery: Supporting provider modernization and innovative care and payment models designed to improve care and strengthen long-term sustainability. The Trump Administration is delivering on its commitment to strengthen rural America by investing in solutions that modernize technology to promote health and well-being for rural communities. Led by CMS, the RHTP is moving beyond the failed one-size-fits-all approach and giving rural communities the tools to build stronger, more innovative, and more sustainable health systems that deliver better care and better outcomes for the Americans they serve.
