Leadership Giving — Beacon Society Maine Public's Corporate Circle Promote Your Business or Organization on Maine Public Donor FAQ Donate a Vehicle Maine Public Store Health Maine awarded more than $190 million for rural health care in second year of federal grant program Maine Public | By Patty Wight Published November 12, 2026 at 3:27 PM EST Facebook LinkedIn Bluesky Email Listen • 1:03 The federal government is awarding Maine more than $190 million to support rural health care.
The state is receiving roughly $350,000 more than it was awarded in the first year of the Rural Health Transformation Program.
According to Gov. Janet Mills' office, the state will use the funding to build on its efforts to boost workforce training and modernize electronic medical records.
Priorities for year two include establishing more school-based health centers and mobile health units, and integrating primary care into behavioral health clinics.
The state says it also wants to support tribal traditional healing practices and better electronic medical records for jails to help ensure residents have seamless care when they return to the community.
The Rural Health Transformation Program is distributing $50 billion to states over five years. It's part of the Big Beautiful Bill signed into law last year that's estimated to cut $137 billion in rural Medicaid spending over the next decade.
