North Dakota’s rural health money allocated but details not yet public Skip to content Share Add Us On Google Add as a preferred source on Google BISMARCK, N.D. (North Dakota Monitor) - The $199 million North Dakota received for the first year of a massive federal rural health care grant has been allocated, though the state says it will likely be weeks before all of the award recipients are public. The money comes from the Rural Health Transformation Program, a $50 billion, five-year funding package approved by Congress last year as part of its budget reconciliation law. The program guarantees every state at least $500 million in grant money. States had until the end of October to obligate all of the money given to them for year one of the program. North Dakota last week announced it met this goal in part by contracting with third party organizations to help with the application and award process. The award recipients have been chosen but contracts are still being finalized, Alysia Huck, the program’s communications manager, said in an email. Specific award amounts are still being decided, she said. “Once those agreements are finalized and signed, we’ll be able to provide a more detailed breakdown of award recipients and funding amounts,” Huck said. Program Director Krissie Meyer said she expects this process to be complete by the end of the year or early January. Some states have already made most of their year one Rural Health Transformation Program grants public. Nebraska, for example, has a dashboard featuring the funds that were awarded and spent through September 30, 2026. North Dakota is set to receive roughly $206.4 million from the federal government for year two of the grant, the state announced last week. The award is about $7.4 million more than it received for the first year — the largest percentage increase of any state. The state received a total of 800 applications for its first year grant opportunities according to the Department of Health and Human Services’ website. The department hasn’t disclosed the total number of award recipients. The grants are split across four initiatives: expanding rural health care access, addressing workforce issues, promoting healthy lifestyles, investing in technology and data. The Department of Health and Human Services recently began working with third party organizations to help process applications and administer the grants. In August, the department made details of about roughly $11.2 million in grants public in a report to the federal government. That included money for hospital staff recruitment and retention, nutrition programs and a mobile dental unit, according to the report. Meyer said the state is considering adding more third-party partners for the second year of the program. While states have already submitted their draft budgets for year two to the Centers for Medicaid Services, the final budgets have yet to be approved. Budget revisions are due by the end of November, Meyer said. Copyright 2026 KVLY. All rights reserved.
