Background
The GMCB must establish a budget for each general hospital by September 
15, with a written decision by October 1. 18 V.S.A. § 9456(d)(1)(A).
The GMCB must establish hospital budgets that are consistent with the 
state’s health care goals as defined in statute.
The GMCB has a process to set hospital budget benchmarks, which can be 
used to guide hospitals in developing and preparing their proposed budgets, 
and as consideration for the Board’s review of proposed budgets. GMCB 
Rule 3.000, § 3.103(a); § 3.203; § 3.305(b)(1).
Hospitals bear the burden of persuasion in justifying their proposed budgets. 
GMCB Rule 3.000, § 3.305(a).
If a hospital does not meet its burden, the GMCB must adjust the budget to 
ensure consistency with the state’s health care goals.
=== PAGE 7
7
Principles for Health Care Reform 
18 V.S.A. § 9371
The GMCB must execute its charge consistent with the principles for health care reform. 
These principles include, in part:
• Universal access to high-quality, medically necessary health services. Systemic 
barriers, such as cost, must not prevent people from accessing necessary care. 18 
V.S.A. § 9371(1).
• Health costs must be contained. Growth in spending must balance the “needs of the 
population with the ability to pay for such care.” 18 V.S.A. § 9371(2).
• The system must be evaluated regularly to improve quality, access, and cost 
containment. 18 V.S.A. § 9371(9).
• Unnecessary expenditures must be eliminated. This includes administrative costs or 
other costs that do not contribute to efficient, high-quality services or improve health 
outcomes. 18 V.S.A. § 9371(10).
• The financing of health care in Vermont must be sufficient, fair, predictable, transparent, 
sustainable, and shared equitably. 18 V.S.A. § 9371(11).
=== PAGE 8
8
General Assembly’s Intent for GMCB
18 V.S.A. § 9372
It is the intent of the General Assembly to create an independent 
board to promote the general good of the State by:
1. improving the health of the population;
2. reducing the per-capita rate of growth in expenditures for health 
services in Vermont across all payers while ensuring that access to 
care and quality of care are not compromised;
3. enhancing the patient and health care professional experience of 
care;
4. recruiting and retaining high-quality health care professionals; and
5. achieving administrative simplification in health care financing and 
delivery.

Hospital Budget Criteria
18 V.S.A. § 9456
For review of a hospital’s proposed budget, the GMCB considers, in part:
• Vermont’s critical health needs and resources; (§ 9456(b)(2))
• Actual hospital performance with respect to past budgets; (§ 9456(c)(5))
• Utilization information; (§ 9456(b)(1))
• The efficiency of the hospital’s operations; (§ 9456(c)(4))
• Hospital administrative costs; (§ 9456(b)(12))
• Financial contributions to a hospital network, and if those expenses are consistent with the 
principles for health care reform; (§ 9456(c)(4))
• The salaries for hospital executives, including variable payments and incentive plans, and if these 
are consistent with the principles for health care reform; (§ 9456(b)(15))
• A comparison of hospital executive salaries to the salaries of the lowest-paid hospital employees 
who deliver direct care; (§ 9456(b)(15))
• Public comment on all aspects of hospital costs and use and on individual budgets; (§ 9456(b)(7))
• The benchmarks established by the Board; (§ 9456(e)(4))

Changes to Hospital Budget Rule: History
• The prior version of Rule 3.000 was adopted in 2013, as required by Act 
171 (2012). The current version was revised, with the process starting in late 
2025 and the revisions being effective June 15, 2026.
• There have been minor amendments to the Board’s budget review statute 
since 2013, but revisions to Rule 3.000 were not necessary.
• Last year there were significant amendments to the Board’s budget review 
authorities in Act 49, Act 62, and Act 68.
• The revisions to Rule 3.000 incorporate amendments to statute, better 
describe the current budget review process, and streamline and simplify 
certain parts of the rule.

Summary of Changes to Rule 3.000 (1)
Redlined Document Showing the Bulk of the Differences (PDF pages 24-39)
Presentation at GMCB Board Hearing on Nov. 5, 2025 going over the changes in detail
Section 3.103 Definitions
• Added or updated the definitions for “benchmark,” “Hospital Budget Guidance,” “Uniform Reporting” 
Manual,” as well as for “general hospital” vs “psychiatric hospital”
Section 3.203 Benchmarks
• The Board may set benchmarks each year (previously the rule used “will establish benchmarks”).
• “Benchmark” is defined as the criteria a hospital can use to develop and prepare their budget. 
(3.103(a))
• Benchmarks are included in the Hospital Budget Guidance (previously included in Uniform Reporting 
Manual). (3.203(a))
• Benchmarks are considered for determining whether to exempt a hospital from public hearing (3.304) 
and during review of a hospital’s proposed budget (3.305) (previously they were used to “determine 
whether a budget may be adjusted” in addition to being a factor for consideration during budget 
review)
=== PAGE 13
13
Summary of Changes to Rule 3.000 (2)
Section 3.205 Hospital Duties; Effect of Noncompliance
• Removed a long list of specific items to be filed each year (for example costs of operation, utilization, 
inpatient services by type) in favor of utilizing the Hospital Budget Guidance and Uniform Reporting 
Manual to specify the required items at this granular of level (3.205(a))
• Clarifies that the Budget Submission Deadline is different for general vs psychiatric hospitals (3.205(a))
• Includes requirement that all filings are also submitted to the Office of the Health Care Advocate (which 
is a statutory requirement) (3.205(b))
• Allows the board to “decline to consider the relevant portions of a hospital’s submission” if the hospital 
fails to file by the deadline, file the required information, file in the manner contained in the Uniform 
Reporting Manual, or submit the filing to the Office of the Health Care Advocate (3.205(c))
Section 3.301 Information Available to the Public
• Clarifies that since the GMCB is a public agency, all nonconfidential information filed by a hospital is 
available to the public upon request, consistent with the Vermont Public Records Act or other law 
(previously the rule used language specific to information filed as part of the budget review process and 
to the Public Records Act alone.)
=== PAGE 14
14
Summary of Changes to Rule 3.000 (3)
Section 3.302 Confidentiality
• Added a brand-new section regarding the procedure for a hospital to request confidentiality of a 
document in accordance with the allowed reasons under the Public Records Act or other law.
• Allows the Board to designate material as confidential (3.302(a))
• Must include the Office of the Health Care Advocate on a confidentiality request (3.302(c))
• Outlines what must be included in a confidentiality request (3.302(d))
Section 3.303 Public Hearings
• Requires public hearings (previously the rule said the Board “may hold public hearings”)
Section 3.304 Exemptions from Public Hearings
• Allows exemption from public hearings for any number of hospitals (previously the limit was four 
hospitals, and exemption was not allowed from the four largest hospitals by net patient revenue)
=== PAGE 15
15
Summary of Changes to Rule 3.000 (4)
Section 3.305 Review Process
• Updated the factors for consideration for a hospital’s proposed budget to reference and reflect the 
statutory requirements (for example the hospital budget review criteria at 18 V.S.A. § 9456 and the 
principles for health care reform at 18 V.S.A. § 9371) (previously the language included some of these 
items but not all)
Section 3.306 Establishment of Hospital Budgets; Decisions
• Breaks out the requirement differences between general hospitals vs psychiatric hospitals
=== PAGE 16
16
Summary of Changes to Rule 3.000 (5)
Section 3.400 Budget Adjustments, Deviations, and Corrective Measures
• Added a process for the optional mid-year budget review by the GMCB of a hospital’s current year 
budget to ensure that a hospital is not likely to significantly deviate from its current year budget (3.401)
• The previous rule conflated the processes for prior-year budget performance review and a hospital’s 
application for adjustment; the new rule separates these processes into two separate sections (3.402 
and 3.403)
• Updated the prior-year budget review process to require the GMCB to account for any significant 
deviations in a hospital’s established budget, unless the hospital demonstrates that it could not have 
reasonably taken action to avoid the significant deviation; the previous process gave more discretion to 
the GMCB to consider factors and allowing the GMCB to determine whether to adjust the hospital’s 
budget or not (3.402)
• Adds a deadline for hospital current-year requests for budget adjustments (3.403(a))
• Requires a showing of need based on an exceptional or unforeseen circumstance in order to adjust a 
hospital’s budget upon request; the previous rule listed several additional factors to consider (3.403(b))

Standard Budget Conditions – DRAFT (1)
Restrictions on Revenue and Price Growth
A. [HOSPITAL]’s FY27 NPR/FPP (“NPR”) budget is approved at a growth rate of not more than [X]% over its FY26 budget, with a 
total NPR of not more than $[X] for FY27.
B. [HOSPITAL]’s FY27 commercial NPR budget, based on either its submitted budget or its budget as adjusted in this Order, is 
approved at no more than $[X]. [HOSPITAL] shall report its actual expected commercial NPR not later than March 15, 2027, or 
such later date as specified by the Director of Health Systems Finance and explain any variations from the expected commercial 
NPR.
C. [HOSPITAL]’s commercial reimbursement rate is approved at not more than [X]% over current approved levels, with no 
commercial reimbursement rate for any payer at more than [X]% over current approved levels. The commercial reimbursement 
rate overall or with respect to any payer may be less than [X]% as negotiated between the hospital and payer. [HOSPITAL]’s 
overall change in charge is approved at the minimum necessary amount to effectuate its commercial reimbursement rate, but 
no greater than the amount identified in its budget submission or its budget as adjusted by this order.
D. This commercial reimbursement rate cap shall apply only to services not capped at 120% ASP for outpatient prescription drugs, 
as defined in Act 55, Sec. 4 (18 V.S.A. § 9407).
E. The commercial reimbursement rate in Paragraph C is a maximum subject to negotiation between [HOSPITAL] and commercial 
insurers. [HOSPITAL] shall not represent the maximum reimbursement rate approved by the GMCB in Paragraph C or the 
commercial NPR based on that rate as an amount set or guaranteed by the GMCB in the hospital’s negotiations with insurers.

CAH Cost-Sharing: Information Gathered
The Issue:
At Critical Access Hospitals (CAHs), Medicare beneficiaries pay 20% of charges rather than 20% of the FFS Medicare 
payment amounts for outpatient services. Hospital charges are often far higher than both the hospitals’ actual costs 
and the Medicare rate, resulting in a potentially significantly higher cost-sharing burden for Medicare beneficiaries 
who receive outpatient services at CAHs.
GMCB RFIs and Public Meetings:
The Board requested additional information from Vermont's eight CAHs and VAHHS to explore the CAH cost-sharing 
issue and potential solutions and held public meetings on this matter throughout February – June 2026.
VAHHS provided a letter to GMCB on June 5th, 2026, that outlined voluntary actions CAHs could take to address the 
cost-sharing issue. Additionally, VAHHS provided a draft patient notification template.
FY27 Hospital Budget Guidance
If your hospital is a Critical Access Hospital, how does your budget address the Medicare cost-sharing issue 
discussed in a board meeting on 3/24/2026 and in the RFI sent out on 2/18/2026?

Guiding Legislation
18 V.S.A. § 9375 states that “[t]he Board shall execute its duties consistent with the principles expressed in section 
9371 of this title.” These principles recognize the need for "universal access to and coverage for high-quality, medically 
necessary health services for all Vermonters” and the need for Vermont's health care system to continuously evaluate 
and improve health care quality and safety. The principles also recognize that cost is barrier to accessing necessary 
health care. 
Specific duties of the Board include:
• Overseeing the development and implementation, and evaluating the effectiveness, of health care payment and delivery system 
reforms designed to “control the rate of growth in health care costs; promote seamless care, administration, and service delivery; and 
maintain health care quality in Vermont . . . .” 18 V.S.A. § 9375(b)(1)(A). 
• Developing and maintaining a method for evaluating systemwide performance and quality, including identifying appropriate process and 
outcome measures. 18 V.S.A. § 9375(b)(10).
• In consultation with VPQHC, utilizing mechanisms in the hospital budget review process to measure hospital costs, quality, and access, 
as well as alignment with the Statewide Health Care Delivery Strategic Plan, once established. 18 V.S.A. § 9456(e)(1). 
• Monitoring the implementation of hospital service eliminations to determine their benefits. 18 V.S.A. § 9456(e)(3). 
• Determining whether new health care projects “will improve the quality of health care in the State or provide greater access to health 
care for Vermont’s residents, or both,” and will “support equal access to appropriate mental health care that meets standardsof quality, 
access, and affordability equivalent to other components of health care as part of an integrated, holistic system of care, asappropriate.” 
18 V.S.A. § 9437(4).

Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP)
• The Rural Health Transformation (RHT) Program was authorized by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Section 71401 of Public Law 119-21) in 
July 2025. It is a $50 billion federal initiative by CMS to strengthen rural healthcare access, quality, and outcomes across the United States.
• The RHT Program seeks to further the following Strategic Goals:
• Make rural America healthy again
• Sustainable access
• Workforce development
• Innovative care
• Tech innovation
• The RHT Program is funded by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS). 
• Vermont’s Year 1 (FederalFiscal year 2026) award is $195 million.
• Important to note that there are claw back provisions and that funding will be recalculated annually, giving the administration the authority to 
claw back funds if states don't pass promised policies or demonstrate adequate progress toward stated goals. NRHA's Rural Health Voices 
Blog | National Rural Health Association - NRHA - NRHA
• Vermont’s RHT Program Goals are:
1. Strengthening Vermont’s Rural Health Care Workforce;
2. Increasing Access to Timely Care Across Rural Communities; and
3. Leveraging Innovative Strategies to Increase Quality and Reduce Health Care Costs.

Overview of Board’s Role Around Hospital 
Proposed Service Changes
In 2025 the Vermont Legislature passed Act 68 (S.126), which gave 
the State a stronger role in protecting patient access when a hospital 
elects to reduce or eliminate services to stay within their established 
budget.
Act 146 of 2026 replaced Act 68, reimagining the process hospitals 
must follow when proposing to eliminate a service line. This new 
process now provides an opportunity for hospitals to confidentially 
consult with the State as they contemplate an elimination and 
requires the hospital to facilitate a community engagement period, 
among other steps, if they decide to move forward with the 
elimination.
