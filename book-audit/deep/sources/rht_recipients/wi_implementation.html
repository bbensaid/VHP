Rural Health Transformation Program

Rural Health Transformation Program: Implementation

Wisconsin’s Rural Health Transformation Program focuses on three major initiatives—Workforce, Technology, and Coordinated Care—that are implemented by a variety of partners.

Program administration

The Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS) leads the implementation of the Rural Health Transformation Program through the Office of Grants Management (OGM). OGM provides technical and operational guidance and resources to administer large scale, federally funded programs designed to improve access to high-quality healthcare in rural communities across Wisconsin.

Program partners

Within DHS, several divisions carry out Rural Health Transformation program work, including the Division of Care and Treatment Services, Division of Medicaid Services, Division of Public Health, Office of Children’s Mental Health, and Tribal Affairs Office.

Outside of DHS, 16 implementation partners are funded to carry out large scale aspects of programs across the state. DHS also funds grantees ranging from small community-based organizations to large hospital systems that applied to implement projects, including community health worker projects, technology allocations, dental technology projects, and coordinated care projects.

In addition, DHS contracts with a variety of entities that can provide needed services and technical expertise to advance the program’s goals.

All 11 federally recognized Tribal Nations in the state receive a set allocation through the program.

Budget Year 1 funding

Budget Year 1 funding supported implementation partners and competitive grant funding opportunities. In addition, funds were allocated to DHS program staff and contractors supporting DHS-led programmatic initiatives. Less than 10% in administrative funds were allocated to the program team.

Please note that all dollar amounts are the obligated awards and are subject to Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) review. This program is reimbursement based; organizations must submit claims for completed work in order to receive payment.

Implementation partners

The 16 implementation partners named in Wisconsin’s original application to CMS (PDF) are uniquely positioned to carry out large scale, specific rural health projects across the state. DHS awarded $79,373,358.70 to its implementation partners for Budget Year 1. Several program implementation partners are announcing funding awards for separate RHT grant opportunities they released over the summer. These include the Department of Public Instruction, Department of Workforce Development, Universities of Wisconsin, and Wisconsin Technical College System.

Competitive grants

In Fall 2026, DHS awarded $69.9 million in grants for Budget Year 1 to organizations to advance the goals of the Rural Health Transformation Program. Organizations submitted funding applications in June and July for projects beginning in October. Awarded projects will impact rural health through advances in technology, investments in the rural healthcare workforce, promoting youth mental health, and the development and scaling of partnerships to improve care coordination for rural communities.

Learn more about the Budget Year 1 competitive grant funds awarded in the Workforce, Technology, and Coordinated Care initiatives.

Advisory council

An advisory council comprised of 23 representatives from rural communities across Wisconsin provides guidance to DHS on the implementation of the program.
