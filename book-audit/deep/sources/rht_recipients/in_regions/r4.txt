Gov. Braun Awards Grant to Transform Rural Healthcare 
 in East-Central Indiana Region 
 
Indianapolis, IN- Gov. Mike Braun announced today that the east central Indiana region 
(Region 4) has been awarded $15.2 million in Growing Rural Opportunities for Well-
being (GROW) initiative grants. GROW region four includes Blackford, Fayette, Grant, 
Henry, Jay, Madison, Randolph, Tipton, Union, Wayne counties.   
 
GROW is focused on improving healthcare access, quality and outcomes in rural 
communities across the state of Indiana and supported by the Indiana Rural Health 
Transformation Program (RHTP), administered by the Indiana Department of Health 
(IDOH) and Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA). Indiana received nearly 
$207 million for the first year of the five-year federal Centers for Medicare and Medicaid 
Services (CMS) grant program. About $120 million, or 60% of that funding, was 
earmarked for the regional grants.  
 
“The Trump Administration’s Rural Health Transformation Program is based on one 
simple idea: your access to quality health care shouldn’t depend on your ZIP code,” said 
CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz. “Funding new approaches to expand access and 
improve health outcomes for rural Hoosiers is an important step toward achieving that 
vision and I look forward to seeing RHTP dollars support equally transformative 
innovations for rural communities nationwide."  
 
“This funding is a major step forward in our commitment to making health care 
affordable and accessible for all Hoosiers,” Gov. Braun said. “It’s an important stride 
toward creating a more sustainable and connected network in rural Indiana.”  
 
Region 4 GROW grant activities include:  
 
• Expand community-based access to care: Build stronger community health 
worker networks, mobile integrated health, rural mobile clinics, and coordinated 
transportation, including a CHW regional network, MIH home-based assessments, 
rural mobile clinic deployments and a regional transportation plan that reduces 
missed appointments and strengthens access across all counties.  
 
  
   
 
 
• Implement a regionwide maternal health access model: Bring 
prenatal/postpartum care into homes and community settings; expand 
navigation, remote patient monitoring, maternal education and other 
direct perinatal support services; and integrate behavioral health/SUD treatment 
into maternal care workflows to address high-risk outcomes.  
• Transform behavioral health and crisis response: Utilize ED embedded 
clinicians, a new adolescent inpatient psychiatric unit, standardized regional crisis 
services messaging and dedicated behavioral health transport, all aimed at 
reducing preventable ED utilization, crisis boarding, and out of region transfers.  
• Strengthen emergency medical response and rural clinical readiness: Expand 
MICU critical care transport and prehospital blood access across 12 counties.  
• Build a long-term rural healthcare workforce pipeline: Support career pathway 
expansion, employer sponsored EMT/paramedic cohorts, externship and clinical 
placement supports, preceptor development, rural training clinics and expansion 
of sonography and other high demand programs.  
 
Rural Health Transformation Program’s GROW will now move into implementation building on a 
foundation of strong collaboration, local leadership and meaningful impact from targeted 
activities.  
 
Region 4’s award complements the seven other regions that received GROW regional grants. 
RHTP also includes 11 statewide initiatives that promote workforce investment, thoughtful 
partnerships and infrastructure development. Additional information about the GROW regional 
grants and 11 statewide initiatives are available on the RHTP website growruralhealth.in.gov.
