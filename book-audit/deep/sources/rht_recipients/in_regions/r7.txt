Gov. Braun Awards Grant to Transform Rural Healthcare 
 in South-Central Indiana Region 
 
Indianapolis, IN-  Gov. Mike Braun announced today that the south-central Indiana 
region (Region 7) has been awarded $13.1 million in Growing Rural Opportunities for 
Well-Being (GROW) initiative grants. GROW region seven includes Brown, Crawford, 
Harrison, Jackson, Lawrence, Orange, Perry, and Washington counties.  
 
GROW is focused on improving healthcare access, quality and outcomes in rural 
communities across the state of Indiana and supported by the Indiana Rural Health 
Transformation Program (RHTP), administered by the Indiana Department of Health 
(IDOH) and Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA). Indiana received nearly 
$207 million for the first year of the five-year federal Centers for Medicare and Medicaid 
Services (CMS) grant program. About $120 million, or 60% of that funding, was 
earmarked for the regional grants.  
 
“The Trump Administration’s Rural Health Transformation Program is based on one 
simple idea: your access to quality health care shouldn’t depend on your ZIP code,” said 
CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz. “Funding new approaches to expand access and 
improve health outcomes for rural Hoosiers is an important step toward achieving that 
vision and I look forward to seeing RHTP dollars support equally transformative 
innovations for rural communities nationwide."  
 
“This funding is a major step forward in our commitment to making health care 
affordable and accessible for all Hoosiers,” Gov. Braun said. “It’s an important stride 
toward creating a more sustainable and connected network in rural Indiana.”  
 
Region 7 GROW grant activities include:  
 
• Build a coordinated provider ecosystem: Create network of community 
paramedicine, remote monitoring, and mobile primary care, behavioral health, 
dental and preventive screening services to reach rural residents.   
 
 
 
  
   
 
 
• Improve maternal and infant outcomes: Expand prenatal/postpartum 
navigation, maternal fetal medicine access, infant supports, perinatal mental 
health programs and a rural-focused OB residency.    
• Increase crisis service access: Medication for opioid use disorder availability, 
mobile crisis teams, school based mental health, youth prevention efforts and 
behavioral health workforce pathways.   
• Address distance-based disparities: Provide transportation support, community 
navigation, digital literacy, housing stability programs, recovery supports and 
linkage to behavioral and medical care.   
• Strengthen long term rural health capacity: Create coordinated pipelines for 
behavioral health, clinical, CHW, peer support and high school–based workforce 
development.   
• Focus on healthy families: Strengthen early prevention, youth resiliency and 
family supports to improve long term mental and behavioral health outcomes. It 
expands evidence-based programs in schools and community settings, increases 
access to infant and early childhood mental health services and enhances family 
centered recovery and stability supports.  
 
Rural Health Transformation Program’s GROW will now move into implementation building on a 
foundation of strong collaboration, local leadership and meaningful impact from targeted 
activities.  
 
Region 7’s award complements the seven other regions that received GROW regional grants. 
RHTP also includes 11 statewide initiatives that promote workforce investment, thoughtful 
partnerships and infrastructure development. Additional information about the GROW regional 
grants and 11 statewide initiatives are available on the RHTP website growruralhealth.in.gov.
