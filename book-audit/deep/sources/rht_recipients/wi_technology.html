Rural Health Transformation Program: Technology

Initiative 2: Interoperability Infrastructure and Modernization

Rural Wisconsin needs the technology to support and reach residents, such as closed-loop referral systems and telehealth capabilities. Wisconsin is investing in upgrades to rural provider systems and digital infrastructure and is developing a digital rural healthcare collaborative.

Driving rural technology and innovation

This initiative helps rural communities connect to and provide care through technological advancements. The technology initiative supports the launch of a statewide electronic health record and allocates funds to rural facilities to upgrade information technology systems, enable telehealth, improve interoperability, and enhance cybersecurity.

By fostering the use of innovative technologies that promote efficient care delivery and expand access to digital health tools, this funding transforms patient rural care, achieves better health outcomes, and improves patient safety. Wisconsin is also launching a teleconsultation line for behavioral health to increase access to timely specialty services in rural areas. Tools help patients connect with health information to manage chronic conditions and access specialty and behavioral health services closer to home.

The technology initiative includes two transformative projects with a significant rural impact, including:

  • Facility technology transformation: This project provides rural facilities with funding to purchase technology to transform patient care. It also supports providers with a digital backbone through the creation of a statewide electronic health record.
  • Public navigation transformation: This funding leverages technology to better connect residents with public services. It includes investments in the state’s community and health information exchanges and the creation of a single platform to navigate state agency resources. It also provides funding to strengthen farmer wellness and increase telehealth access for veterans.

Program work and updates

Learn more about the Rural Health Transformation Program projects and work currently underway. Please note that all dollar amounts are the obligated awards and are subject to Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) review.

Rural Technology Transformation Funds to Providers

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $39,740,002

DHS will allocate funds to rural facilities to purchase technology that removes barriers to care, maximizes provider productivity, and ensures patients benefit from modern digital health tools. Funds will be distributed to a broad base of rural providers to ensure that technology advancements reach every corner of Wisconsin. Facilities can leverage this one-time investment to adopt emerging health technology focused on supporting rural populations. DHS released the GFO for the first round of Rural Technology Transformation Funds in July 2026. DHS awarded $39,740,002 in funding to 33 organizations. The first round of funds are allocated to healthcare organizations with one or more Rural Health Clinics and healthcare organizations with one or more Federally Qualified Health Centers serving rural and semi-rural communities. A second round of funding will be announced in 2027.

Rural Health Care Collaborative: Statewide Electronic Health Records (EHR)

Lead agencies: Department of Health Services (DHS), Epic
Budget Year 1 award amount: $29,000,000

Wisconsin is building a digital collaborative that gives rural providers the opportunity to join a shared digital backbone, enabling them to upgrade clinical systems. Participating providers will gain secure access to a comprehensive, longitudinal health record for each patient, chronic disease management tools, a patient-facing portal, population-level rural health data dashboards, telehealth services to support access to an expanded care network, a closed-loop community service referral network, and other tools. Through a competitive procurement process, DHS selected Epic as the vendor to build the statewide EHR. DHS is selecting the first cohort of provider organizations to join the statewide system.

Rural Dental Efficiency and Access Grants

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $9,500,722

DHS awarded grants through a competitive grant funding opportunity to dental clinics to adopt efficient cleaning technologies and expand routine dental services for rural Medicaid beneficiaries. New technologies help provide faster and more comfortable cleanings, maximizing the dental workforce by allowing providers to see more patients per day. This increases access without having to recruit more providers. Wisconsin aims to increase the efficiency of current Medicaid providers and incentivize dentists to enroll as Medicaid providers by subsidizing the purchase of these efficient technologies. Organizations submitted applications in July and DHS awarded $9,500,722 to 37 organizations in Fall 2026.

Information Exchange Investments – 211 and WIRE

Lead agency: United Way of Wisconsin
Budget Year 1 award amount: $2,000,000

Through its statewide network and deep local relationships, United Way will align health systems, community-based organizations, and local leaders around shared goals to appropriately utilize the Wisconsin Information and Referral Exchange (WIRE) and 211 community helpline. This strategy will accelerate sustainable, community-led solutions that improve access to care and overall health outcomes in rural Wisconsin. United Way of Wisconsin is working with Wisconsin Statewide Health Information Network (WISHIN) to connect WISHIN’s patient index to WIRE, identify underrepresented services in the 211 directory, and offer a sub-grant opportunity for local United Ways. Two local United Ways have been identified for this opportunity, and additional United Ways may apply. An additional staff person has been hired and planning meetings with technology vendors and community partners have been held. Outreach to healthcare associations has begun, and a pilot program for closed-loop referrals is being developed.

Information Exchange Investments – WISHIN

Lead agency: Wisconsin Statewide Health Information Network (WISHIN)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $499,078

WISHIN will invest in strengthening the statewide health information exchange (HIE) network by modernizing its platforms through four projects: (1) enabling Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR), which will allow participants to use this emerging industry standard; (2) developing a data quality process to identify areas for improvement from contributors; (3) Managing Enterprise Master Patient Index (eMPI) worklists to ensure proper patient matching in a platform upgrade; and (4) hiring an external expert to apply for HITRUST certification. WISHIN is currently evaluating vendors and partners to support this work as their agreement with DHS is being finalized.

Integrated State Platform: No Wrong Door for Mental Health

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $9,398,000

Wisconsin will create a new website that will help rural residents seeking mental health resources and services from state agencies, connecting the dots across multiple state agencies and entry points. The project, currently in the discovery phase, will improve customer service by creating efficiencies by embracing self-service, digital-first, and modern technologies. This includes ensuring all individuals can access digital services, emphasizing data to drive service delivery, and enhancing shared governance across state agencies.

Farmer Wellness Program

Lead agency: Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection (DATCP)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $195,384

DATCP is strengthening its Farmer Wellness Program to address the farmer mental health crisis. One-time investments will improve the user experience for farm families and increase awareness and participation in the program. Counseling providers will receive training on voucher programs to make payments more accurate and timely. Enhanced outreach will increase the number of farmers and farm families receiving counseling care. Funds have been used to support the counseling voucher program to meet growing demand for vouchers. DATCP is also working to modernize voucher processing infrastructure. DATCP has launched an educational campaign to promote the Farmer Wellness Program, including public service announcements delivered through radio, social media, email, and direct mail. Photos and video footage have been created to use in future campaigns.

Veteran's Telehealth

Lead agency: Wisconsin Department of Veterans Affairs (WDVA)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $1,000,000

WDVA is administering one-time funds that will help the state’s county and Tribal Veterans Services Offices provide access to telehealth in safe and convenient spaces through the creation of private telehealth rooms. Funds will help offices purchase technology, remodel spaces for privacy, and educate veterans on using technology. WDVA plans to award grants to 20 counties each year. Listening sessions with County Veterans Service Officers have been held to identify technology and access gaps and to review other needs.
