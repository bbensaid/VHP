SC awards 228 grants with Rural Health Transformation Program funding

by Blakesley Rhett

Tue, September 15, 2026 at 12:50 PM
SC awards 228 grants with Rural Health Transformation Program funding. (WACH)

COLUMBIA, S.C. (WACH) — Gov. Henry McMaster announced on Tuesday the recipients of the Rural Health Transformation Program funding.

South Carolina has awarded 228 grants totaling $167,299,900.69 in funding to strengthen healthcare access, quality and outcomes in primary care, maternal and infant care, pediatrics and behavioral health in rural communities.

These grants provide one-time funding for high-impact, sustainable healthcare initiatives that remove systemic challenges and build infrastructure and long-term capacity through facility modernization, technology upgrades and workforce development.

“Every South Carolinian should have access to quality health care close to home, no matter where they live. South Carolina is working to ensure rural communities have the resources they need to provide that care, and this is another important step in strengthening health care across our state," McMaster said.

Among the funded projects are an expansion of multi-year efforts to strengthen partnerships between local law enforcement and the Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities for mobile crisis response; cybersecurity upgrades for a rural, independent hospital to prevent further cyberattacks and optimize billing and a specialized surgical robot to enhance outcomes for mothers and babies in complicated deliveries.

“The Trump Administration’s Rural Health Transformation Program is helping states like South Carolina develop innovative, locally driven solutions to improve rural Americans’ access to high-quality care. These grant awards demonstrate how this historic investment is empowering rural communities to strengthen their health systems, put patients first, and bring better care closer to home," said Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz.

Designated as the lead state agency for this program by McMaster, the South Carolina Department of Health and Human Services received 712 applications for this initiative by the June 1, 2026, deadline. Awardees were selected through a multi-level merit review scoring process, with rules and funding limitations established by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

"We are proud of the strong interest and innovative ideas brought forth to strengthen rural health. This funding will help bring these ideas to life and make a lasting difference in rural communities for years to come," said SCDHHS Director Eunice Medina.
“Thank you, Governor McMaster, Director Medina, and the Health and Human Services staff for your transformational leadership and exemplary approach for this funding opportunity. Your vision and investment in rural South Carolina hospitals and healthcare providers is deeply meaningful and will help strengthen healthcare infrastructure, expand access to care, and bring innovative healthcare solutions closer to rural South Carolinians and communities. This is about more than improving healthcare infrastructure—it’s about reimagining care, removing barriers to care, and ensuring our neighbors have access to high-quality healthcare close to home, while building a stronger and more sustainable future for our community," said John Snow, CEO of Newberry Health.
“The foundation of healthcare is access to quality, comprehensive, and preventive primary medical care. As a grant recipient, our independent family medical practice will be better able to serve our community. For 38 years, we have been delivering babies and caring for families in the office, hospital, and nursing home. If South Carolina hopes to improve health outcomes, we must start by transforming rural healthcare. This grant program will leverage local rural public-private partnerships and build healthier communities. We are thankful to Governor McMaster and SCDHHS Director Medina for their leadership in bringing hope and real help to our community," said Dr. Oscar Lovelace, Founder of Lovelace Family Medicine
“What has long motivated our work is the fundamental belief that a person’s ZIP code should not determine his or her access to healthcare or define his or her quality of life, and that is why we are so thankful for this invaluable investment in our rural communities. Thanks to this funding, we have an incredible opportunity to bring trusted community partners and healthcare providers together. When we collaborate, we can break down barriers to care, meet people closer to home and help build a stronger, more connected rural healthcare system for South Carolina," said Kathy Schwarting, CEO of Palmetto Care Connections.
