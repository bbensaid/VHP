Rural Health Transformation Program

Rural Health Transformation Program: Workforce

Initiative 1: Rural Talent Recruitment and Retention

Recruiting and retaining a robust healthcare workforce is a challenge in rural areas, making access to quality, timely care for rural residents difficult. Wisconsin’s Rural Health Transformation Program invests in innovative workforce projects in rural communities, supports career pathways for rural healthcare providers, and funds a pilot project to boost the adoption of community health workers.

Strengthening the rural healthcare workforce

To address provider shortages, this initiative helps train new healthcare workers, recruit workers to rural areas of need, and retain workers in those roles. The workforce initiative does not provide funding to individual students or medical professionals. Rather, DHS and implementation partners are making strategic investments to increase the sustainability of systems beyond the five-year program. These investments will maximize the impact of funding and contribute to positive health outcomes in rural communities over the long term.

By building sustainable partnerships between educators and clinics, expanding rural training opportunities, and strengthening community-driven solutions, this funding contributes to the development of a thriving rural healthcare workforce in Wisconsin. Additionally, strategies that address recruitment challenges for rural areas, such as housing and childcare deserts, are crucial parts of the workforce framework.

The workforce initiative supports workforce development projects across the full pipeline, including:

  • Workforce readiness: From middle school to medical school, equip educational partners with tools to grow the next generation of healthcare professionals through allocations to educational institutions.
  • Rural workforce grants: Empower communities to develop regionally driven solutions through competitive grants.​
  • Community health worker integration: Pilot community health worker integration, study the return on investment, and sustain support through Medicaid coverage starting in 2028.

Who we're working with

  • Rural healthcare providers
  • Community health centers
  • Behavioral health clinics
  • Dental providers
  • Long-term care providers
  • K-12 education institutions
  • Medical, dental, and nursing schools
  • Community colleges and higher education
  • Local and Tribal health departments

Program work and updates

Learn more about the Rural Health Transformation Program projects and work currently underway. Please note that all dollar amounts are the obligated awards and are subject to Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) review.

Rural Workforce Grants to Providers

Lead agency: Department of Workforce Development (DWD)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $15,888,343.35

DWD is administering competitive grants for rural workforce development partners to implement innovative projects to address their region’s most pressing rural health workforce challenges. Funding supports projects with leading-edge, long-term solutions that help employers find workers, and help workers prepare for and connect to better, higher-quality, and more family-sustaining jobs. DWD created the Workforce Innovation Grant: Healthcare Employment, Access, and Rural Transformation (WIG:HEART) program and has begun hiring to support the program. DWD awarded funds to selected grantees in Fall 2026.

Community Health Workers Grants to Providers

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $19,942,572

DHS awarded funds to strengthen the state’s rural health workforce by developing and expanding high-quality and impactful community health worker (CHW) programs in clinical and community settings. Grantees will receive funding to hire and support CHWs to carry out services in rural communities, train CHWs on core competencies and standards, and build organizational capacity to sustain quality CHW service provision. DHS awarded funding to 49 organizations to implement CHW programs.

Workforce Conditions Study

Lead agency: University of Wisconsin Population Health Institute (UWPHI)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $250,000

UWPHI Envision will conduct a workforce conditions study that includes overall health care systems, technology, and workforce facilitators and barriers. This study will support the development of the Medicaid State Plan Amendment (SPA) for community health worker services. This includes contracting with national community health worker evaluation experts and learning from other states who have implemented reimbursement mechanisms and have existing SPAs.

Cost, Implementation, and Impact Study

Lead agency: University of Wisconsin Population Health Institute (UWPHI)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $500,000

UWPHI will evaluate the implementation and impact of the five-year community health worker program. Grantees of the CHW Rural Workforce Grants will be required to participate in the study to demonstrate the successful implementation, impact, and scope of CHW services. The study will support the sustainability of the CHW program by highlighting the program components that are most effective and efficient for improving rural health.

Medicaid State Plan Amendment

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Medicaid Services (DMS)

Informed by CHW workforce grants and the CHW impact study, Wisconsin’s DMS within DHS will add CHWs as a reimbursable service when delivered under the direction of a physician or other medical professional, as allowable under federal law. The state will seek authority from the federal Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) through a state plan amendment (SPA) and will develop reimbursement rates, define eligible services, and establish billing policies to help leverage services in an effective, evidence-based way. DMS has conducted a landscape assessment to gather information about social, economic, and health realities in our state. DMS has also researched other state CHW benefits and meets regularly with partners to engage CHWs and develop a new CHW benefit for Wisconsin. DHS anticipates that Wisconsin CHW services will be reimbursable under Medicaid by 2029.

Professional Development and Training

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $274,714

DHS awarded funds through a competitive grant opportunity to training providers to develop, provide, and evaluate high-quality training, education, and professional development for community health workers and community health worker supervisors as essential components of workforce sustainability. Grants support two main components: core competency training and support for community health workers, and community health worker supervisor training and support.

Technical Assistance

Lead agency: University of Wisconsin Population Health Institute (UWPHI)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $1,500,000

Experts at UWPHI Envision will provide direct technical assistance to grant recipients of the CHW pilot program. Technical assistance will include supporting CHW program development and best practices, financial sustainability, and strategic planning. UWPHI will also support grantees to assess their organization’s readiness, offer quality improvement, and develop regional or statewide training and immersion-style learning opportunities based on needs assessment results. Strategic education, advising, and collaboration on CHW-related policies and practices will inform the creation and implementation of the Medicaid CHW State Plan Amendment.

High School – AHEC

Lead agency: Wisconsin Area Health Education Centers (AHEC)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $1,000,000

The investment in Wisconsin AHEC will contribute to the next generation of health care through expansion of: clinical health career exploration opportunities for rural middle and high school students, community-based learning opportunities to rural pre-health and health profession college students, CHW training and CHW registered apprenticeship programs in rural communities, and awareness of loan assistance programs available to rural health providers in Wisconsin. AHEC has held statewide planning meetings and Community Health Immersion events. They’ve hired six staff to expand rural health career exploration opportunities, and they’ve built systems to track engagement and outcomes. AHEC has also coordinated CHW training programs, including a Spanish-language program, and provided professional development opportunities for current rural CHWs.

High School – DPI

Lead agency: Department of Public Instruction (DPI)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $1,000,000

DPI awarded funds to 21 school districts through a competitive grant program for K-12 schools to create or strengthen health science career pathways for high school students in rural communities. Funding will also support the growth of Wisconsin Health Occupations Students of America (HOSA)–Future Health Professionals to engage rural students in intra-curricular leadership development, community health initiatives, and competitive skill events. DPI hired staff to support this program. Grantees are being announced in Fall 2026.

Higher Education – Universities of Wisconsin

Lead agency: Universities of Wisconsin (UWSA)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $22,587,792

The Universities of Wisconsin System Administration (UWSA) are investing in clinical training opportunities, simulation labs, and health care programs across rural Wisconsin through UW campuses. Campuses across the state submitted project proposals in response to a grant funding opportunity released this summer. Announcements are being made in Fall 2026. Funds will cover initial accreditation fees, instructor training and compensation, curriculum design, and recruitment outreach. Funding will also support hospitals with residency programs and equip rural sites with tools like technology for tele-precepting and patient-care documentation systems. UWSA hired staff to oversee this initiative, and 16 projects from 12 universities were selected for funding. These projects plan to establish eight new programs, expand eight existing programs, and create 15 clinical training opportunities and seven simulation-based learning experiences. This will strengthen 65 community partnerships that support Wisconsin’s rural health workforce pipeline.

Higher Education – WTCS

Lead agency: Wisconsin Technical College System (WTCS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $22,587,792

WTCS is investing in evidence-based clinical training opportunities, simulation labs, and health care programs across rural Wisconsin. WTCS implemented a non-competitive allocation process to 16 technical colleges to support new and expanded rural clinical training, simulation labs, curriculum development, equipment, facilities, and accreditation. These allocations will support nursing, behavioral health, dental therapy, and allied health programs. WTCS held a kickoff meeting with all 16 technical colleges and established a dedicated mailbox and monthly meetings to help with ongoing program coordination. Programs are expected to become self-sustaining after the initial start-up period through tuition, fees, and other funding sources. WTCS also hired staff to support this project. 

Rural Dentist Residency

Lead agency: Marquette University School of Dentistry (MUSOD)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $5,000,000

MUSOD is developing a rural residency program for dental students and partnering with rural hospitals to recruit dentists to high-need rural areas. MUSOD launched dental residency internship projects, which included hiring staff, identifying program and clinical sites, and seeking accreditation. Curriculum has been developed for non-categorical internships, and the curriculum for a full residency program is underway.

Wisconsin Psychiatry Extension (WISCOPE)

Lead agency: Medical College of Wisconsin (MCW)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $900,217

MCW has established and implemented the Wisconsin Psychiatry Extension (WISCOPE) program, an adult psychiatry access program that provides teleconsultation on behavioral health services. Consultation is available to providers such as physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, and registered nurses across all areas of practice including family medicine, internal medicine, obstetrics and gynecology, and behavioral health. MCW hired staff and formed a team, including external partners, to develop this program. Communications efforts to bring awareness to this program are underway, as program expansion, outreach, and training also continue.
