Governor DeWine Announces $92 Million to Expand Access to Quality Healthcare in Rural Ohio

October 16, 2026

(SANDUSKY, Ohio)—Ohio Governor Mike DeWine and Dr. Mehmet Oz, administrator of the U.S. Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS), today announced Rural Health Transformation Program awards totaling $92 million to manage chronic illnesses and expand access to preventive, primary, maternal, and behavioral healthcare in rural areas. 

“These investments will transform healthcare in rural Ohio,” said Governor DeWine. “We are grateful to Dr. Oz, CMS, and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services for standing up this important program and partnering with us to ensure that all Ohioans, regardless of where they live, have access to the care they need.”

"Thanks to the Trump Administration's Rural Health Transformation Program, we're proud to work with state and local partners to turn bold ideas into lasting improvements for rural communities like those here in Ohio," said CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz. "I want to thank Governor DeWine for his leadership in bringing local leaders and health care providers together to build stronger partnerships. Together, we can Make America Healthy Again."

The awards, administered by the Ohio Department of Health (ODH), include:

  • $31.4 million to establish or expand Clinically Integrated Networks (CIN) and Rural Health Innovation Hubs
  • $30.3 million to improve maternal care
  • $30.3 million in one-time investments to 22 rural hospitals

“Our goal is to provide the best and most timely patient care as close to home as possible, and to create networks that effectively coordinate quality preventive and follow-up care, all of which are essential for the best patient care,” said ODH Director Dr. Bruce Vanderhoff.

Rural Hospital Investments

Twenty-two rural hospitals are receiving a one-time investment of $1,377,479 for specific quality-improvement projects. For example, some projects include opening behavioral health units to support access to substance use disorder treatment and mental health services; adding on-site MRI services; adding or expanding diagnostic services in areas where current capacity is limited or patients are placed on waitlists. These investments allow rural hospitals to improve the quality of patient care and bring services to patients in their own communities.

  • Holzer Medical Center in Gallipolis (Gallia County)
  • Joint Township District Memorial Hospital in St. Marys (Auglaize County)
  • Knox Community Hospital in Mt. Vernon (Knox County)

Clinically Integrated Networks (CIN) and Rural Health Innovation Hubs

CINs and Rural Health Innovation Hubs serve as networks for physicians, hospitals, clinics, emergency medical services (EMS), behavioral health centers, pharmacists, institutions of higher education, community resource centers, and other healthcare providers. Their focus is to create robust care models that provide high-quality care at a reasonable cost.

Today's announcement was made at Firelands Regional Medical Center in Sandusky, which is a member of the Ohio High Value Network, one of the CINs that is being awarded funding.

“This award recognizes what independent rural hospitals can accomplish when we work together,” said Jeremy Normington-Slay, Vice Chair, Ohio High Value Network, and President and CEO, Firelands Health. “Through the Ohio High Value Network, nearly 3 million rural Ohioans will benefit from coordinated care that is stronger than any one of us could deliver alone. We are grateful to CMS and the State of Ohio for their partnership and their confidence in our approach.”

Awardees include:

  • Beavercreek Medical Center (Soin/Kettering Health) - $15,470,879
  • Child & Family Health Collaborative of Ohio - $6,709,515
  • Mercy Health - St. Rita's Medical Center (Lima) - $325,005
  • Ohio Association of Community Health Centers - $10,406,623
  • Ohio High Value Network - $24,980,798
  • Ohio State Health Network - $3,401,944
  • University Hospitals Health System - $725,702

Maternal Care
Six awardees are focused specifically on improving prenatal, postnatal, and maternal care. For example, funds will be used to provide mobile prenatal care services, offer virtual specialty care for pregnant women, and increase capacity at rural hospitals to allow more premature and medically-vulnerable newborns to receive appropriate care closer to home.

Awardees include:

  • Beavercreek Medical Center (Soin / Kettering Health) - $1,427,200
  • Mercy Health - St. Rita's Medical Center (Lima) - $50,000
  • Ohio Association of Community Health Centers - $1,836,462
  • Ohio Health Institute - $3,630,000
  • Ohio High Value Network - $24,952,221
  • Ohio State Health Network - $2,084,117

Governor DeWine announced in December 2025 that Ohio had received more than $200 million from the federal government in RHTP awards. Previously, Governor DeWine announced two rounds of RHTP funding: $10 million to improve the rural health workforce and $3.15 million to help expand rural pharmacy connectivity.

This project is supported by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) as part of a financial assistance award totaling $92 million with 100 percent funded by CMS/HHS. The contents are those of the author(s) and do not necessarily represent the official views of, nor an endorsement, by CMS/HHS, or the U.S. Government.

More details can be found on ODH’s Rural Health Transformation website.

*Ohio has been awarded $92 million in both Fiscal Years 2026 and 2027 for the hospital CIN/healthcare innovation hub initiative. The awards announced today include all of the FY 2026 funding and a portion of the FY 2027 funding, which will be available beginning October 31, 2026.
