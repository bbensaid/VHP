Rural Health Transformation Program

Rural Health Transformation Program: Coordinated Care

Initiative 3: Population Health Infrastructure

Rural Wisconsinites often experience a lack of coordination between healthcare providers when they seek care. Whether they see their primary care doctor or behavioral health provider, visit a hospital or specialist, or receive support from a community-based organization, patients benefit from a coordinated approach. This initiative supports and strengthens partnerships that create strong, sustainable, and community-centered healthcare delivery systems.  

Transforming rural care through partnerships

This initiative includes three major projects that support innovative models of care and invest in under-resourced areas of Wisconsin’s health system, including: 

  • Care coordination grants: Transform delivery through innovative care models developed through regional partnerships. Examples include mobile clinic development, telehealth partnerships, service co-location, and co-responder models for behavioral health.
  • Behavioral health innovations: Address Wisconsin’s rural mental health crisis, especially for kids, starting in 2026.
  • Medicaid reform and other strategic investments: Invest in Tribal Nations, Medicaid reforms, and public intervenor services to improve health care for patients and providers.

By facilitating regional coordinated care networks and investing in services in rural communities, this initiative ensures care is provided in the right setting, by the right provider, at the right time. Funding promotes improvements in primary and behavioral health care by leveraging telehealth and teleconsultation services, deploying mobile clinics, and developing co-located models of care. Additionally, this initiative invests in modernizing behavioral health data and reporting systems, advanced payment reform and financial stability for rural providers. 

Each of the 11 federally recognized Tribal Nations in Wisconsin will receive $500,000 per year ($27.5 million total) to implement the three Wisconsin Rural Health Transformation Program initiatives in their communities. Tribal Nations’ community-based organizations are also eligible to apply for grants and opportunities in each of the three initiatives.

Program work and updates

Learn more about the Rural Health Transformation Program projects and work currently underway. Please note that all dollar amounts are the obligated awards and are subject to Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) review.

Care Coordination Grants to Providers

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS)

DHS is administering competitive grants to support new and innovative partnership models that coordinate care across systems and sectors in rural Wisconsin. These grants will improve rural access to preventative services, primary care, dental services, and behavioral health services through team-based care models delivered in trusted sites close to home. DHS will award up to $10 million in 2026 for a six-month planning period through a GFO released in June. Awardees will be invited to submit a full application for a four-year program period. The full grant opportunity will be released to planning grant recipients in Spring 2027, with an estimated $25 million available in the first year pending CMS approval.

Behavioral Health Study

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Care and Treatment Services (DCTS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $150,000

DCTS is administering a comprehensive study of the current behavioral health system landscape to enhance access in rural areas, ensure equitable and quality services statewide, improve care coordination across services, and create reimbursement efficiencies. DCTS is contracting with a consultant to develop recommendations to enhance access, ensure quality care, and improve care coordination, especially in rural areas.  

Data Portal (PPS) Upgrade  

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Care and Treatment Services (DCTS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $3,708,794.62 

DCTS is administering one-time funding to modernize the Program Participation System (PPS), an online application for Tribal nations and counties to submit data to DHS. The project will develop a new state data system for all 72 county Mental Health and Substance Use (MH/SU) agencies and all 11 federally recognized Tribal nations to submit client service utilization data paid by Medicaid and other funding sources. Funding will also include training and support for counties and Tribal nations to transition their electronic health records system to the new DHS system. A vendor has been selected and system specifications have been developed. 

Youth Peer Support Curriculum  

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Care and Treatment Services (DCTS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $198,000

DCTS is administering funding for the Youth Peer Support Curriculum Initiative to adapt an existing curriculum for adult peer support. The adapted curriculum will have three on-ramps. For individuals under the age of 18, it will provide essential tools for youth-focused peer support under the guidance of a teacher or counselor in schools or congregate care settings. For young adults (approximately ages 18-30), it will provide essential tools plus additional education to become a peer recovery coach or certified peer specialist in Wisconsin. For existing peer recovery coaches or certified peer specialists, it will provide additional education for a specialization in providing services to youth. DCTS is collaborating with UW-Milwaukee Center for Innovative Transition, Education & Employment to develop the curriculum and identify a rural school district to pilot it. An advisory committee has been established and includes representatives from the Department of Public Instruction, Cooperative Educational Service Agencies (CESAs), Providers and Teens Communicating for Health (PATCH), and Youth Move. Steering committee meetings and youth listening sessions have also been held.

Student Leadership Summit 

Lead agency: Office of Children’s Mental Health (OCMH)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $5,000

OCMH's annual Mental Wellness Student Leadership Summit brings schools with school-based student-led mental wellness programs together in Madison to provide mental health education, foster relationships, and inspire school-based wellness programming. Funds were used to support expansion and enhancements for the April 2026 summit, with participants from 55 schools, 350 student leaders, and 100 adult advisors. There was a 72% increase in participation of rural students and a 53% increase in rural adult advisors this year.

Rural School Mental Health Training 

Lead agency: Office of Children’s Mental Health (OCMH)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $25,000

OCMH released a competitive bid to develop and deliver training tailored for behavioral health professionals working in rural and semi-rural schools to better equip them to address the mental health needs of students. The Wisconsin Association of Family and Children’s Agencies has been selected to conduct the training. An advisory team, including clinical experts, student services professionals, and higher education professionals has begun meeting, and have identified priority topics for trainings anticipated for January, February, April, and May 2027.

Expansion of Sources of Strength

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Public Health (DPH)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $499,921

DPH awarded funds through a competitive grant process to expand Sources of Strength (SOS) programming in rural Wisconsin schools. SOS is a best practice, strength-based suicide prevention program offered in schools. The program is designed to increase wellbeing, help-seeking, resiliency, healthy coping, and belonging. DHS awarded $499,921 in the first year of funding to Cooperative Education Service Agency (CESA) 6 to coordinate train-the-trainer certification for school staff, and to support the long-term sustainability of SOS in rural Wisconsin schools.

Fully Integrated Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans (FIDE SNPs)

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Medicaid Services (DMS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $500,000

To expand utilization of FIDE SNPs, Wisconsin will prioritize education for partners, advocates, and members about the benefits of integrated care. Wisconsin will develop education and resources to help Medicaid members and providers understand Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans: what they are, the benefits they provide, and the options available in Wisconsin. DMS has selected a marketing vendor to develop materials to support this education and communication. Hard copy, web, and other materials will focus on several audiences including: Income Maintenance workers, Aging & Disability Resource Center specialists, enrollment brokers, and members. The goal of this effort is to create a sustainable framework for helping members make an educated choice about integrated care and promote enrollment in FIDE-SNPs for members who may benefit from this integration. 

Rural Health Clinic Payment System Conversion

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Medicaid Services (DMS)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $398,365

DMS is partnering with rural health clinics on an initiative to shift from cost-based reimbursements to a modernized prospective payment system (PPS). A one-time investment from DMS will cover costs needed to move all clinics to a modernized PPS methodology to reflect current prices and reduce payment delays. Medicaid will leverage Rural Health Transformation funds to prepare for payment changes effective in calendar year 2028. DMS has created a dedicated team with experience leading a similar transformation effort for Wisconsin Tribal Federally Qualified Health Centers and has begun communicating the timeline and process for the PPS transition for impacted providers. DMS hired eight staff and re-aligned existing teams to set up Rural Health Clinic support for long-term success. DMS has kicked off projects related to rates, systems, and communications to support this conversion. DMS also met with partners to identify key stakeholders.

CARES Modernization Study

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Medicaid Services (DMS)

DMS will invest in a study to evaluate modernization options for Wisconsin’s eligibility management system, known as CARES, which supports Medicaid, Supplemental Nutrition Assistance, and Temporary Assistance for Needy Families. The system faces challenges in cost efficiency, disaster recovery, regulatory compliance, and maintainability. Medicaid will evaluate modernization options that will improve performance, reduce risk, and align with future goals. This will help rural residents maintain access to secure governmental services. DMS issued a request for service for a vendor to evaluate the CARES system and make recommendations to modernize. DMS has defined the specific scope of work for this vendor, including cross-agency collaboration needs. 

Health Navigator Services

Lead agency: Office of the Commissioner of Insurance (OCI)
Budget Year 1 award amount: $3,000,000

OCI is contracting for public intervenor services to help rural patients and providers maximize insurance coverage and payments. Navigators will help patients obtain insurance coverage, connect to in-network providers, secure necessary prior authorization approvals, and resolve claim denials. Financial performance specialists will help health care providers manage revenue cycles and reimbursement. Through a proof-of-concept, the state will explore sustainable support through a provider or insurer-supported fee-based model. The project focuses on two areas: enrollment or connecting people to a medical home, and helping people navigate insurance issues, including denials. Subgrantees have been selected. OCI finalized statements of work and interagency agreements with Covering Wisconsin, who will focus on enrollment, and the Center for Patients Partnerships (University of Wisconsin Law School), who will have a hotline and help people with insurance issues. OCI has hired two project positions and has established regular planning meetings between OCI, Covering Wisconsin, and the Center for Patients Partnerships. Covering Wisconsin has served hundreds of Wisconsinites with enrollment services and The Center for Patients Partnerships has selected a marketing firm to carry out a public intervenor service campaign.

Tribal Nations Allocations

Lead agency: Department of Health Services (DHS)

Each of the 11 federally recognized Tribal Nations within Wisconsin will receive $500,000 per year, totaling $27.5 million over five years, pending CMS approval and funding. These funds will be used to implement the three Wisconsin Rural Health Transformation initiatives in their communities. Tribal Nations can leverage this transformational investment to modernize IT systems, build a culturally competent workforce, and innovate care delivery to meet the unique needs of members. Ten Tribal Nations began implementing workplans; Tribes have hired staff, held competitive bid processes for services and equipment, and built relationships with partners in their communities.
