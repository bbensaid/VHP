## Claims checked against their own source (MATCH/MISMATCH + proposed fix)

### MISMATCH 1 — "Implications for You" attributes the $1,303 admin gap to CAHs [1744] — CRITICAL
> "If you are a Vermont hospital executive: The $1,303 per-discharge administrative
> cost gap **between Vermont's CAHs** and the national benchmark is not a compliance
> problem…"

Figure 11.3 and Figure 11.4 both attribute $1,303 to **PPS** hospitals
($2,730 − $1,427). The CAH row is an **operating-profit** row with a **−$1,846**
gap. This is the same class of error found in Chapter 1: the prose contradicts the
cells of the figure it is quoting. It is also self-contradicting within the
chapter — [1603] and [1607] both say PPS.
**Proposed fix:** "The $1,303 per-discharge administrative cost gap between
Vermont's PPS hospitals and the national benchmark…"

### MISMATCH 2 — legislator section attributes $2,730 to CAHs [1746] — CRITICAL
> "Vermont's administrative cost per adjusted discharge — **$2,730 for CAHs** versus
> $1,427 national benchmark — is the most precise measurement of administrative
> waste available…"

Figure 11.3: $2,730 is **PPS** management/admin cost. CAHs have no admin figure in
the chapter at all. Second instance of the same PPS→CAH swap.
**Proposed fix:** "…$2,730 for PPS hospitals versus $1,427 national benchmark…"

### MISMATCH 3 — Figure 11.9 benchmark for the CAH gap is $2,759, not $2,784 [1737]
Row "Revenue cycle optimization and HCC coding" reads "Vermont CAH gap:
$938/adj discharge vs. **$2,759** benchmark (RHRC data)". Figure 11.3 gives
**$2,784**. The $938 matches; only the benchmark drifts, by $25. Also note the
$1,846 gap asserted in [1602] is derived from $2,784, so $2,759 is the outlier.
**Proposed fix:** change `$2,759` to `$2,784` in the Figure 11.9 cell. (Attributing
it to "RHRC data" while Fig 11.3 sources the same pair to the AHS November 2025
report is a second, smaller inconsistency; recommend dropping "(RHRC data)".)

### MISMATCH 4 — Figure 11.9 presents UVMMC's $3,826 as statewide "Vermont" [1737]
Row "Administrative simplification" reads "**Vermont**: admin cost $3,826/adj
discharge vs. $1,427 national benchmark". $3,826 appears exactly once elsewhere —
Figure 11.5's **UVM Medical Center** cell, where it is explicitly hospital-specific
("OW specific finding"). The statewide PPS figure the whole chapter is built on is
$2,730. As written, Figure 11.9 asserts a statewide admin cost 40% higher than
Figures 11.3 and 11.4.
**Proposed fix:** "Vermont PPS hospitals: admin cost $2,730/adj discharge vs.
$1,427 national benchmark (UVMMC $3,826)".

## Other issues

### Competing superlatives (internal contradiction of the Chapter-1 class)
Five different things are each named the most/primary X of the Operations pillar:

- The **$1,303 admin gap** is "**the primary** financial opportunity" [1558] and
  "**the primary** operational inefficiency target" [1607].
- The **CAH operating profit gap** is "**the most urgent** financial signal" [1602].
- **Transfer infrastructure** is "**the most visible** immediate need" [1625].
- **Workforce redistribution** is "**the most sensitive** operational challenge"
  [1628] — while **workforce** as a whole is "Vermont's **Most Binding** Operational
  Constraint" [1630] and "**most likely to constrain** the pace and ambition of
  transformation" [1633].
- **Administrative shared services** make "**the most immediate** impact on the
  $400M+ savings target" [1627] while "**some of the largest** near-term cost
  savings" [1627].

"Most urgent signal" / "most visible need" / "most sensitive challenge" / "most
binding constraint" are arguably different axes, and the chapter never says so.
**Recommended:** keep at most one unqualified superlative per axis and make the axis
explicit in the others ("the most urgent *financial* signal", "the most *politically*
sensitive"). The one pair that is a genuine collision is [1558]/[1607] ("the
primary financial opportunity" / "the primary operational inefficiency target",
admin gap) against [1602] ("the most urgent financial signal", CAH profit gap) —
