# Slide 1 — Title

## Transforming American Healthcare

### A Six-Pillar Framework for System Transformation

*Vermont as the National Teaching Case · v28 · 2026*

> Open by naming the central claim: American healthcare is not just expensive — it is structurally failing, and reform is not optional. The book makes the case that transformation across **six pillars together** is the only path that works.

---

# Slide 2 — The starting premise

American healthcare:
- $4.5 trillion annually
- Bottom-ranked outcomes among wealthy nations
- 40+ years of failed cost-containment attempts
- A workforce in burnout collapse
- Coverage gains being actively reversed (H.R. 1, 2025)

> The book opens with a Preface — "A System at the Breaking Point" — that argues incremental adjustment can no longer substitute for transformation.

---

# Slide 3 — Why "Six Pillars"?

Most reform efforts move **one pillar at a time**:

- A new payment model (Economics, alone) — fails without measurement (Technology)
- A new EHR (Technology, alone) — fails without clinical redesign (Clinical)
- A new clinical model (Clinical, alone) — fails without payment alignment (Economics)
- A new equity initiative (Equity, alone) — fails without operational integration (Operations)

> Transformation requires the pillars to move **together**. That is the book's structural claim.

# Slide 5 — Chapter 1: The 15 Dependencies

Six pillars × five other pillars ÷ 2 = **15 pairwise dependencies**.

The book maps each one explicitly — e.g.:
- Technology → Economics (you can't pay for value without measurement)
- Policy → Clinical (clinical models can't operate outside legal mandates)
- Equity → all five others (equity is a structural input, not a downstream filter)

> The 15-dependency map is the analytical instrument that supports the entire book. It is also a tool on the HTR platform (`/about/framework`).

# Slide 6 — Chapter 2: Why Order Is Not Optional

**The execution sequence:**

1. Policy
2. Technology
3. Economics
4. Clinical
5. Equity
6. Operations

> The book argues this order is not preference — it is causal. You cannot pay for value (Economics) without measurement (Technology). You cannot demand measurement without a mandate (Policy).

# Slide 7 — The OneCare Vermont Autopsy

Chapter 2's central case study: **Vermont's ACO experiment, OneCare**.

- Launched in 2017 as an all-payer ACO
- Failed by 2024 — multi-hundred-million-dollar losses
- Diagnosis in the book: **economics without technology readiness**

> OneCare tried to assume value-based risk before Vermont had the data infrastructure to measure care quality across the system. Chapter 2 reads this as a sequencing failure, not a strategy failure.

---

# Slide 8 — Chapter 3: Sequencing in Practice

Three principles for execution:

1. **Critical path** — what must happen before everything else
2. **Parallel work** — what can move in parallel without blocking the path
3. **Equity as a design constraint** — not as a final overlay

> Vermont's Act 68 timeline (2025–2030) is read as a worked example of these three principles.

# Slide 10 — Vermont Acts 167 & 68

**Act 167 (2022):** Mandated a comprehensive system diagnostic. Produced the Oliver Wyman report. Set the agenda.

**Act 68 (2025):** Operationalized the agenda. Hospital global budgets. Reference-based pricing. Implementation through 2030.

> The two acts together are the most legislatively complete state-level health transformation effort in the United States. The book treats this combination as the central teaching case.

# Slide 12 — H.R. 1 and the Medicaid landscape

H.R. 1 (signed 2025):
- Federal Medicaid work requirements
- Tighter eligibility verification
- Coverage cliff for an estimated 12–18M nationally
- Vermont impact: smaller (Vermont has comparatively few work-requirement-eligible enrollees), but ripple effects on state budgets significant

> The book argues H.R. 1 is the most important Medicaid policy shift of the decade — and the most underappreciated.

# Slide 16 — Reference-Based Pricing

The pricing architecture that **precedes** global budgets.

- Anchors hospital payments to a transparent reference (e.g., a multiple of Medicare)
- Ends the era of opaque chargemaster economics
- Vermont's strategy under Act 68 builds reference-based pricing as a foundation

> Without reference-based pricing, global budgets have no defensible starting point.

# Slide 19 — VCCI in Chapter 10

**Vermont Chronic Care Initiative** — intensive case management for the top 5–15% of Medicaid members.

# Slide 20 — SASH in Chapter 10

**Support and Services at Home** — Vermont's most operationally mature aging-population response.

- 200+ communities, 13,000+ participants
- Documented hospitalization reduction
- Lower per-member Medicare spending

> SASH is the proof of concept for **care-where-people-live** under global budgets — when avoided hospitalization is the explicit objective, not a revenue threat.

# Slide 29 — Chapter 17: The Vermont / Oregon / California Triangulation

Three states represent **three points on the transformation spectrum**:

# Slide 31 — California CalAIM

$6.7 billion Medi-Cal transformation. 1115 waiver. 15 million members. 57 counties.

- **Enhanced Care Management (ECM)** — intensive care for homeless, recently-incarcerated, SMI, LTC populations

> The largest whole-person care experiment in the United States. The 2026+ outcome data will be the strongest evidence base any state's transformation has ever generated.

# Slide 45 — Major themes (4/4) — Operations as the failure mode

The book's most contrarian claim:

Most transformation efforts have correct policy, decent technology, plausible economics, and good clinical models — but die in **operations**:
- Revenue cycle unprepared for global budgets
- Workforce strategy misaligned with new care models
- HCC coding accuracy too low to support risk contracts
- Administrative costs too high to absorb the new payment regime

> Chapter 14–15 is the book's antidote.

# Slide 46 — What the book is NOT

- It is **not** a polemic — it engages with each pillar seriously
- It is **not** a Vermont memoir — Vermont is the case, not the subject
- It is **not** a "how to lobby Congress" book — federal policy is treated as a boundary condition
- It is **not** an academic literature review — it is practitioner-grade synthesis
- It is **not** speculative — it stays close to documented evidence

> What it **is**: an operating manual for state-level health system transformation, grounded in Vermont's live experiment.

# Slide 52 — Key concept — Failure Cascade

Defined in Chapter 1, recurring throughout.

> A gap in one pillar produces compounding failures across the other five.

Example: A weak Technology pillar produces:
- Economics failures (cannot pay for value)
- Clinical failures (no risk stratification)
- Equity failures (no SDOH integration)
- Operations failures (no revenue cycle automation)
- Policy failures (no measurement to enforce)

> The failure cascade is why the 15-dependency map is non-negotiable.
