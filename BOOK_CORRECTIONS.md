### ERROR 1: CalAIM Funding Amount

**Location:** HTR_Book_Updates_v28.md, UPDATE 4 — Chapter 17, CalAIM subsection (line 93)  
**Also:** Any occurrence in the main book body where CalAIM funding is mentioned.

**Wrong text (exact quote from Update 4):**
> "California's CalAIM (California Advancing and Innovating Medi-Cal) initiative — a $6.7 billion Medi-Cal transformation approved through a federal 1115 waiver"

**What is wrong:** The $6.7 billion figure has no confirmed primary source. The CMS-approved funding breaks down as approximately $4.3 billion for HCBS and $1.85 billion for the PATH initiative, totaling ~$6.15 billion.

**Correction:** Replace "$6.7 billion" with "approximately $6 billion" — or, if precision is required, cite the specific CMS-approved waiver amounts: "$4.3 billion in HCBS investment plus $1.85 billion for the PATH initiative."

**Action in Update 4 document:** Change line 93 from:  
`a $6.7 billion Medi-Cal transformation`  
to:  
`an approximately $6 billion Medi-Cal transformation`

**Sources:**
- CMS 1115 Waiver documentation (CalAIM approval): https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/section-1115-demo/demonstration-and-waiver-list/waivers_facets/California_CalAIM
- California LAO CalAIM fiscal analysis: https://lao.ca.gov/

### ERROR 3: Vermont Act 55 Effective Date

**Location:** Main book body — chapter covering drug pricing policy/economics. Not present in HTR_Book_Updates_v28.md (this error is in the original docx only).

**Wrong text:**
> "Act 55 outpatient drug cap at 120% of ASP effective April 2025"

**What is wrong:** Act 55 was signed in June 2025. The cap implementation date is January 1, 2026. "April 2025" is the ASP pricing reference date used in the calculation methodology — it is NOT the date the cap takes effect. These two dates are being conflated.

**Correction:** Change to:  
"Act 55 outpatient drug cap at 120% of ASP (using the April 2025 ASP as the reference price), effective January 1, 2026"

**Sources:**
- ILSA Inc. bulletin on Vermont Act 55 (June 2025): https://www.ilsainc.com
- VTDigger coverage, June 12, 2025: https://vtdigger.org

### ERROR 5: H.R. 1 Enactment Date Inconsistency

**Location:** Main book body — appears in multiple places. Some references say "May 2025," others correctly say "July 4, 2025."

**Wrong text (wherever it appears):**
> "Federal legislation enacted May 2025 implementing $911 billion in Medicaid cuts"

**What is wrong:** May 22, 2025 was the date H.R. 1 passed the House (215–214). The Senate passed it July 1, 2025. President Trump signed it into law on **July 4, 2025** (Public Law 119-21). "May 2025" is the House passage date, not the enactment date.

**Correction:** Replace every instance of "enacted May 2025" with "enacted July 4, 2025." Any reference to "May 2025" should clarify it was House passage, not enactment.

**Sources:**
- White House press release, July 4, 2025: https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2025/07/president-trumps-one-big-beautiful-bill-is-now-the-law/
- Congress.gov H.R. 1 bill history: https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1/all-info

### ERROR 6: Vermont Medicaid Share of Insured Population

**Location:** Main book body — Chapter 5 / policy chapter, AHEAD discussion.

**Wrong text:**
> "Vermont's Medicaid program — which covers approximately 19% of the state's insured population"

**What is wrong:** Vermont's Medicaid program covers approximately **24%** of the state's population (roughly 140,400 enrollees). The 19% figure appears to be an error — it may be a fraction of the Medicare population or an older/lower estimate. Confirmed payer mix: Medicare ~23%, Medicaid ~24%, Commercial ~50%, Uninsured ~3%.

**Correction:** Change to "approximately 24% of the state's population" (or verify against current DVHA enrollment data for the most precise figure).

**Sources:**
- GMCB FAQ on Vermont payer mix: https://gmcboard.vermont.gov/all-payer-model/frequently-asked-questions
- Vermont Medicaid enrollment: https://healthinsurance.org/medicaid/vermont/

### ERROR 7: Maryland HSCRC Establishment Date

**Location:** Main book body — Chapter 8 / economics chapter, Maryland All-Payer Model section.

**Wrong text:**
> "Maryland's Health Services Cost Review Commission (HSCRC) was established in 1972 with statutory authority to set hospital rates for all commercial payers"

**What is wrong:** Two errors in one sentence:
1. The HSCRC was created by the Maryland legislature in **1971**, not 1972.
2. Rate-setting authority began in **July 1974** (after a 3-year phase-in). Medicare and Medicaid were added via a federal waiver in **1977**, creating the true all-payer model.

**Correction:** "Maryland's Health Services Cost Review Commission (HSCRC) was established in 1971, began setting hospital rates in 1974, and achieved true all-payer status — including Medicare and Medicaid — through a federal waiver in 1977."

**Sources:**
- HSCRC About Us page: https://hscrc.maryland.gov/Pages/About-Us.aspx
- HSCRC All-Payer Hospital System overview: https://hscrc.maryland.gov/documents/pdr/GeneralInformation/MarylandAll-PayorHospitalSystem.pdf

## PART 4 — ADDITIONAL FLAGS FOR AUTHOR REVIEW (Chapters 13–17)

---

### FLAG E: PMI "Pulse of Profession" Year

**Book says:** "PMI's Pulse of the Profession study (2023) found that organizations with mature project management practices waste 28 times less money"

**Issue:** The "28 times less money wasted" statistic originates from PMI's **2017** Pulse of the Profession report, not the 2023 edition. The 2023 report focuses on different topics (power skills, etc.). This stat has been widely recycled in industry literature citing the older report.

**Action:** Change "(2023)" to "(2017)" or verify against the actual 2023 report content.

---

### FLAG F: Healthcare = 14% of Vermont Workforce

**Book says:** "Healthcare employs approximately 14% of the workforce"

**Issue:** BLS May 2024 data (via Pennsylvania Office of Rural Health multi-state comparison) puts Vermont's healthcare workforce share at **~13%**, not 14%.

**Action:** Change to "approximately 13%" or cite a Vermont-specific source that confirms 14%.

---

### FLAG G: AHEAD "EAST Fund" Terminology

**Book says:** References to "Vermont's AHEAD EAST Fund investments in primary care"

**Issue:** The label "EAST Fund" does not appear in any official Vermont health reform documentation found (GMCB, AHS, CMS). Vermont's AHEAD model includes primary care investment components, and the RHT Program separately funds primary care — but "EAST Fund" may be an internal working title or a conflation of two separate funds.

**Action:** Verify the exact official name of this fund against the AHEAD State Agreement (signed January 2025) and use the official terminology throughout.

---

### FLAG H: AHEAD Covers "23% of Vermont's Insured" (Medicare FFS)

**Book says:** "AHEAD applies to Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries — approximately 23% of Vermont's insured population"

**Issue:** 23% = all Vermont Medicare enrollment. Of that, ~31% are in Medicare Advantage (which AHEAD does not cover). AHEAD's Medicare FFS beneficiaries represent approximately **16%** of Vermont's insured population, not 23%.

**Action:** Clarify: "AHEAD applies to Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries — approximately 16% of Vermont's insured population (with the remaining ~7% of Medicare enrollees in Medicare Advantage plans not covered by AHEAD)."

---

### FLAG I: VBC Penetration "~58% of Commercially Insured"

**Book says:** "approximately 58% of commercially insured Americans in some form of value-based arrangement"

**Issue:** HCPLAN's rigorous 2023 measurement shows ~39% of commercial payments in meaningful APM arrangements. 58% may be defensible only under the broadest possible definition of "some form of VBC" (including pay-for-performance with no risk). If the book uses 58%, it must define what qualifies as a VBC arrangement and cite the specific source.

**Action:** Specify the source and definition. If using AHIP's broader survey, say so. If using HCPLAN, use ~39%.
